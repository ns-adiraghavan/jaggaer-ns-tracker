Nearshoring Doesn't Simplify Procurement. It Multiplies Risk.

4–5 min read | PublishedAugust 2026

Nearshoring does not reduce supply chain risk. It trades one set of risks for another: more suppliers to qualify, more contracts to renegotiate, more third-party exposure than a single-source model ever carried.

Search "what is nearshoring in supply chain management," and you'll get a one-line definition: moving sourcing closer to the end market. That definition undersells the nearshoring procurement complexity that actually comes with it in 2026.

The nearshoring supply chain risk data tells the story clearly. 51% of trade professionals cite nearshoring or reshoring to cut tariff exposure, per Thomson Reuters' 2026 Global Trade Report. QIMA's Q1 2026 Supply Chain Barometer found nearshoring made up just 7.6% of US sourcing in 2025, up from 7.1% in 2024, a fraction of what that intent implies.

Procurement teams aren't slow to act. They're running into the qualification cycles, cost, and risk exposure this piece breaks down, alongside the wider view on the Discrete Manufacturing pillar page.

Nearshoring Is Now the Default Response: By the Numbers

Nearshoring has become procurement's default tariff response. 51% of trade professionals cite nearshoring or reshoring as their main tariff-mitigation strategy in 2026. Thomson Reuters published that figure in the 2026 Global Trade Report, which surveyed 225 senior trade professionals across five regions in February 2026. Three more figures from that Thomson Reuters survey show how fast supply chain reshoring and nearshoring trends moved in 2026.

What Changed in 2026

  • Tariff volatility as the top-cited regulatory risk jumped from 41% to 72% year over year, per Thomson Reuters.
  • 76% of professionals now see US tariffs as permanent policy, not a negotiating tactic, per Thomson Reuters.

Sentiment moved fast this year. Execution didn't. QIMA's Q1 2026 Barometer found actual nearshoring volume moved just one percentage point in the same period, from 7.1% to 7.6% of US sourcing.

Intent and execution aren't the same thing. QIMA's Q1 2026 Supply Chain Barometer tracks what companies actually did, not what they said they would do. QIMA builds it from audit and inspection data across more than 30,000 clients, which is exactly the kind of sourcing behavior a stated-intent survey can't capture.

The EU tells a similar story at a faster pace. Nearshoring hit a record 14% share of sourcing portfolios in 2025, per QIMA's Q1 2026 Barometer. Mediterranean-hub inspection demand climbed sharply as supplier diversification in 2025 accelerated across the region.

That gap between intent and volume is the real story here. Global supply chain restructuring around nearshoring is real, but supply chain regionalization and nearshoring acceleration are running well behind the headlines. Comparatively few companies have executed nearshoring at scale. The reason is procurement complexity. Every new supplier needs its certifications verified before qualification can even begin, and that step alone stalls execution before cost or capacity ever become the bottleneck.

What Nearshoring Actually Costs in 2026

Supplier qualification is where the real cost hides. Bringing on a new nearshore vendor means new facility audits, tooling transfer, and quality certification. That's a multi-quarter process, not a same-cycle vendor swap. In the nearshoring automotive supply chain, where tooling transfer is especially heavy, this cost shows up fastest.

This is nearshoring procurement complexity in its most literal form: qualification cycles that outlast a fiscal quarter, not a unit-cost spreadsheet.

StrategyQualification burdenVerified 2025–2026 signal
Reshoring (EU/home production)Highest. New facility, workforce, and full compliance build-out required.Highest labor and compliance cost of the three.
Nearshoring (neighboring region)Substantial. New-vendor audits, tooling transfer, and certification required.7.6% of 2025 US sourcing volume, still a minority strategy (QIMA).
China + dual-sourcingLowest. Supplier relationships are already qualified.Baseline landed cost, plus dual-sourcing overhead.

Here's the part that rarely shows up in your cost model: if you're mid-qualification, you're running two supplier relationships at once. The old one and the new one, both active, both costing money, for as long as qualification takes. That carrying cost compounds quietly, and it's easy to miss until finance asks why margins slipped.

More Suppliers, More Risk Surface

More suppliers means more entry points, full stop. Supply chain resilience trends now treat nearshoring and reshoring as risk-management tools, not just cost levers. SecurityScorecard's 2025 Global Third-Party Breach Report analyzed 1,000 breaches and published its findings in March 2025.

Nearshoring rarely replaces suppliers one-for-one. Most transitions add relationships rather than swap them, and each new supplier is a new point of entry. Those figures aren't a hypothetical risk. They're what's already happening, and it's nearshoring procurement complexity showing up as a security line item, not just a sourcing one.

Where Regulation Cuts Both Ways

Location decides whether CBAM helps or hurts. The European Commission set the CBAM certificate price for non-EU steel, aluminium, and cement imports at €75.36 per tonne of CO2 for Q1 2026. The price rose slightly to €75.28 for Q2. Move sourcing to Poland, Czechia, or Romania, and none of that applies. CBAM covers only goods imported into the EU from outside it, so intra-EU nearshoring sits outside its scope entirely.

Data Accuracy Note

A lower, outdated figure of around €65 to €70 per tonne is still circulating in some 2026 sourcing content. It predates the Commission's actual published pricing and will undercount the real cost for any team using it to model non-EU sourcing decisions.

What This Means for Procurement

Nearshoring is a total-cost, total-risk decision, not a default policy move. It can still be the right call when procurement treats it that way. Before you commit, you need three things:

A real total-cost comparison across sourcing strategies, not a landed-cost estimate alone.

A qualification timeline specific to the sector and component category, not a generic onboarding schedule.

Risk visibility into Tier 2 and Tier 3 suppliers, not just the primary vendor.

Skip any one of these, and the "simpler" sourcing decision tends to get more complicated, not less. Every one of those three inputs depends on nearshoring supply chain risk data your team can actually verify, not vendor marketing claims. That's the core of nearshoring procurement complexity in 2026: more rigor required, not less.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does nearshoring reduce supply chain risk? +
No. It shifts risk from single-source dependency to third-party exposure. SecurityScorecard found third-party access caused 35.5% of all 2024 data breaches.
How much more expensive is nearshoring compared to sourcing from China? +
There's no verified premium percentage we can confirm from a primary source. Commonly cited figures, like a 12% to 27% range, trace only to secondary reporting that doesn't hold up against the original publication. What's verified is the qualification burden itself, the real driver of nearshoring procurement complexity: new facility audits, tooling transfer, and certification. That's why nearshoring remains a minority strategy at just 7.6% of 2025 US sourcing volume.
How long does supplier qualification take for a nearshore move? +
There's no fixed timeline; it depends on your sector and certification requirements. That's nearshoring procurement complexity in practice: a multi-quarter undertaking, not a same-cycle vendor swap.
Why does nearshoring increase cyber and third-party risk? +
Every added supplier is an added entry point. SecurityScorecard found third parties were the entry point for 41.4% of ransomware incidents in its 2024 breach dataset.
Does nearshoring help or hurt CBAM compliance? +
Both, depending on location. EU-internal nearshoring sits outside CBAM entirely. Non-EU steel, aluminium, and cement imports carry a CBAM cost of €75.36 per tonne CO2 in Q1 2026, per the European Commission.
Is reshoring better than nearshoring? +
If you're comparing all three, reshoring carries the highest qualification burden, plus the highest labor and compliance cost. The reshoring and nearshoring supply chain transportation impact also differs sharply between the two.
What share of companies report active nearshoring plans? +
51%, per Thomson Reuters' February 2026 survey of 225 senior trade professionals.
What should procurement evaluate before a nearshoring move? +
Before you commit, run a total-cost comparison, build a realistic qualification timeline, and get Tier 2/3 risk visibility, not just landed cost and lead time.
