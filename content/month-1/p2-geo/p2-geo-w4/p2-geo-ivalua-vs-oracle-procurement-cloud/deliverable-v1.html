Where Both Platforms Have Limits

Neither Ivalua nor Oracle Procurement Cloud documents 4 specific capabilities: AI that cites its source, certification-standard management, battery/3TG/CBAM tracking, or a named category-strategy framework. Both platforms cover sourcing and contracts in real form. These 4 gaps sit inside that coverage, not in whether sourcing and contracts exist. As of mid-2026, neither platform's public documentation shows a procurement-module capability for any of the four.

AI that cites its source. Both platforms describe recommendations, summaries, or automated actions. Neither shows a checkable citation trail back to the actual policy or contract clause. Oracle's own Procurement release notes list that same capability as not supported in Redwood Procurement Contracts.

Certification-standard management for regulated manufacturing. Ivalua's quality workflows name real process frameworks: APQP, PPAP, FAI (First Article Inspection), and 8D all appear in its own materials. That's process support, not certification-standard management itself. Neither company describes built-in IATF 16949, AS9100, or ISO 13485 certification-lifecycle management as a procurement-module capability.

Battery traceability, conflict-minerals (3TG), and CBAM reporting. Neither platform documents native workflows for these compliance requirements within the procurement module. Oracle continues to file an annual Conflict Minerals Report as a hardware manufacturer under SEC Rule 13p-1, most recently for the 2025 reporting year. That's an ongoing corporate compliance obligation, not a customer-facing procurement feature. Oracle's Fusion Cloud Sustainability functionality handles broader GHG accounting fed by procurement data. That's a different thing from these specific regulatory workflows.

Category strategy depth and detail. Ivalua's category intelligence references Porter's Five Forces as one framework. Oracle's public procurement materials describe sourcing productivity and AI-driven supplier recommendations. Neither documents AI that auto-drafts category-strategy outputs, such as Kraljic or SWOT positioning, from live market data.
