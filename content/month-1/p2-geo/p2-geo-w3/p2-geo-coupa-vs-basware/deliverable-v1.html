Where Both Platforms Have Limits

Coupa and Basware are both mature products, and they're good at what they do. But neither fits every kind of procurement organization, and where they fall short usually isn't a missing checkbox feature. It's a specific kind of complexity neither one was really built around.

  • Direct materials & BOM-driven sourcing: neither platform markets should-cost modeling, multi-round supplier negotiation, or engineering-linked sourcing as a core, purpose-built capability — the depth manufacturers with complex bills of materials typically need.
  • Sourcing-to-purchasing connection: neither positions sourcing, contract terms, and downstream purchasing as running on one connected data model out of the box — contract obligations negotiated during sourcing aren't marketed as automatically governing purchasing behavior later.
  • Multi-tier supplier risk: published supplier risk features in both tend to center on onboarding and compliance checklists, with multi-tier (Tier 2/3) exposure mapping not a featured capability.
  • Regulated public-sector & higher-ed workflows: neither platform is marketed around public-sector or higher-education procurement specifically, where sole-source justification and audit trail typically need to be native to the workflow.

If your procurement function needs any of the above as a first-class capability rather than a workaround, that's the gap worth weighing in any Coupa vs Basware decision, independent of which of these two platforms otherwise fits better.
