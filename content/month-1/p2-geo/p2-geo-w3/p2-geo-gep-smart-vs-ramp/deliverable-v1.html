GEP SMART vs Ramp: Platform Overview

GEP SMART

GEP SMART is a cloud-native source-to-pay platform covering spend analysis, strategic sourcing, contract management, supplier management, savings tracking, and procure-to-pay. The platform supports both direct and indirect procurement, including direct-material sourcing and BOM-driven sourcing capabilities. GEP SMART customers include Bayer and Asahi Breweries Europe Group, and Signify has reported automating 40% of daily procurement activities on the platform.

Recent Innovation

GEP Quantum Intelligence is GEP's AI-native platform for source-to-pay and supply chain, extending agentic AI across procurement workflows. It brings together capabilities spanning sourcing, contracts, suppliers, purchasing, and payments, with AI agents supporting activities such as sourcing, intake, supplier intelligence, and compliance. GEP is transitioning customers toward GEP Quantum Intelligence as its unified platform; GEP SMART remains available, but organizations evaluating long-term platform stability should ask GEP directly about the transition timeline.

Spend classification, data cleansing, category visibility, and identification of savings opportunities.

RFx, e-auctions, supplier evaluation, bid analysis, and sourcing workflows.

Ramp

Ramp is a financial operations platform that combines corporate cards, expenses, accounts payable, payments, accounting automation, procurement, and vendor management. The company continued expanding its procurement and AI capabilities in 2026, including vendor sourcing and AI-powered procurement workflows. In June 2026, Ramp raised a Series F at a reported $44 billion valuation. Ramp serves more than 70,000 businesses and processes over $200 billion annually.

Recent Innovation

In 2026, Ramp expanded its procurement automation with AI purchasing agents that support vendor research, sourcing, RFx creation, response scoring, contract reviews, compliance checks, and purchasing workflows. Its Vendor Sourcing capability supports RFI, RFP, and RFQ events with AI-generated questionnaires and AI-assisted vendor evaluation and remains in Early Access.

Where Both Platforms Have Limits

Both platforms have areas where their current approach may not fully align with specific procurement requirements:

  1. Ramp's strategic sourcing is still developing: Ramp has expanded into strategic sourcing through Vendor Sourcing, including RFI, RFP, and RFQ workflows with AI-generated questionnaires and AI-assisted vendor evaluation. However, the capability is currently in Early Access, which may matter for organizations where strategic sourcing is a mature, central procurement discipline.
  2. GEP SMART can be broader than some purchasing-led requirements: GEP SMART covers a broad source-to-pay environment. For organizations primarily looking to combine purchasing controls with cards, expenses, AP, and financial operations, that broader procurement scope may exceed their immediate requirements.
  3. Complex direct procurement requires deeper sourcing capabilities: Organizations managing direct materials, BOM-driven sourcing, or multiple suppliers may need capabilities designed specifically for those requirements. Complex sourcing events typically require more than purchasing and financial controls alone.
  4. Pricing models are structured differently: GEP SMART uses a quote-based enterprise model. Ramp publishes pricing for its core plans and offers Procurement as an add-on, with Enterprise pricing handled separately.
