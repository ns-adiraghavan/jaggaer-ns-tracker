How to Improve RFP Process: A Step-by-Step Guide

10 min read | PublishedOctober 2026 | Published ByJason Rydzewski

Learning how to improve RFP process performance starts with the process, not the template. According to The Hackett Group's 2026 Procurement Key Issues Study, procurement workloads are projected to rise 8% in 2026 while headcount and operating budgets decline. Every RFP event now competes for fewer buyer hours.

A repeatable 7-step framework improves outcomes more reliably than another redrafted template. Most RFP problems trace back to avoidable process gaps: vague scope, too many invited suppliers, weak evaluation criteria and no feedback loop. The framework applies whether you run a handful of strategic RFPs a year or dozens of routine ones.

This guide is for procurement and sourcing teams that already run RFPs and want to formalize how they work. It covers the 7 steps, the practices behind strong programs, common mistakes and how to write an RFP that draws comparable responses. Automation has a bounded role within the source-to-contract workflow: software removes administrative drag, and people keep the award decision.

Foundations

What Is the RFP Process?

The RFP process is the structured sequence a buying organization uses to define a complex requirement, invite proposals and select a supplier on weighted criteria.

CIPS RFP guidance describes an RFP as a structured process for clear communication and fair competition, not just a document.

For the distinctions between request types, see RFP vs RFQ vs RFI. The RFP itself sits inside the wider strategic sourcing process, after spend and market analysis and before contracting.

Step by Step

7 Steps to Improve the RFP Process

These RFP process steps follow the order of a sourcing event, so each step strengthens the inputs to the next. Pilot them on 1 category, measure the result, then standardize.

  1. 01
    Step 1 · Plan

    Clarify Scope and Objectives Before Drafting

    Write each requirement as an outcome with measurable specifications, not as a description of the incumbent supplier's product. Separate mandatory requirements, which act as pass or fail gates, from scored preferences. For example, an IT services RFP can make ISO 27001 certification mandatory and score committed incident response times.

    Assign a single process owner before drafting begins. Clear ownership is the foundation of RFP process management, because one person controls the timeline, the document and every supplier contact. That owner gets budget holders to sign off requirements before release, since late changes force addenda and reset supplier timelines.

    Deloitte's 2025 Global Chief Procurement Officer Survey ranks siloed ways of working as the top barrier to procurement value delivery, cited by 57% of CPOs. Bring technical, finance, legal and risk stakeholders in while the scope is written, not at the award meeting.

    57%CPOs citing siloed ways of working as the top barrier to value deliveryDeloitte, 2025
  2. 02
    Step 2 · Market

    Narrow the Field With an RFI First

    Use a request for information (RFI) to qualify the market before the full RFP goes out. The CIPS Procurement and Supply Cycle places the RFI at the supplier-selection stage, where it reveals supplier size, capabilities, financials, strengths and weaknesses.

    The RFI also sharpens your own requirements before you commit them to the RFP. Use the responses to:

    Screen

    Check financial stability, compliance status and capacity.

    Shortlist

    Invite only suppliers that can credibly deliver.

  3. 03
    Step 3 · Plan

    Set a Realistic Timeline

    Build the timeline backward from the date the contract must start, and size the supplier response window to the requirement's complexity. Compressed deadlines can limit both participation and response quality.

    Schedule before release Firm Q&A deadline Evaluator availability Approval gates Contract review
  4. 04
    Step 4 · Design

    Build a Weighted Evaluation Matrix Before Issuing the RFP

    Fix the evaluation criteria, weights and scoring scale before the RFP is released. Weight price, quality, delivery, risk and ESG according to category priorities.

    Weighted criteria Price Quality Delivery Risk ESG

    Define what a score of 1, 3 and 5 means for each criterion. Without anchored scales, 2 evaluators can read the same response and reach different scores. A locked matrix also defends the award if a losing bidder challenges it.

  1. 05
    Step 5 · Evaluate

    Score Independently Before Group Discussion

    Each evaluator should score independently first, then calibrate with the panel, so the award reflects evidence rather than the most senior voice. CIPS tender evaluation guidance states that bids should be checked against the criteria set out in the tender, following the same process for every bid. CIPS describes the evaluation team as one with the necessary skills, such as financial, technical, purchasing and legal expertise.

    Require a written justification for every score. For complex awards, run a best and final offer (BAFO) round only after technical scoring is complete.

  2. 06
    Step 6 · Improve

    Automate the Repetitive Administrative Work

    Automate RFP process tasks that consume buyer hours without requiring judgment. Strong candidates include:

    Automate Approval routing Chasing missing attachments Compliance declaration checks Score aggregation

    Automation should never decide the award. Evaluators still read proposals, weigh trade-offs and recommend the supplier.

  3. 07
    Step 7 · Improve

    Close the Loop With Feedback to Every Respondent

    Give every respondent feedback, then feed your own lessons into the next event. CIPS describes feedback to unsuccessful tenderers as good practice and calls for a clear, documented audit trail from start to finish. Base feedback on the published criteria and each supplier's own scores.

    Track 4 metrics for every RFP: cycle time, supplier response rate, savings against baseline and post-award supplier performance. Record which questions produced unusable answers, then update the category template. That retrospective loop shows teams how to improve RFP process quality event by event.

A clear sequence is only half the job. Execution quality decides the outcome.

Best Practices

RFP Process Best Practices That Separate Strong Programs from Average Ones

Strong RFP programs treat sourcing as a managed capability, not a series of one-off documents. Three RFP process best practices show how to improve RFP process maturity in that sense.

ISM's Strategic Sourcing maturity models assess sourcing processes as an organizational competency, alongside spend management and supply chain resilience.

Limit the supplier shortlist

Invite only suppliers your evaluation panel can assess properly. A long list dilutes evaluator attention and lowers the quality of every review. At program level, a disciplined shortlist keeps the supplier base manageable and makes performance comparable across events. Track invited versus responding suppliers per event to check whether the shortlist is sized correctly.

Score total cost of ownership, not line-item price

Evaluate what the contract will cost over its life, not the lowest unit price. A logistics RFP, for example, should model fuel surcharges and accessorial fees alongside base lane rates. Keep commercial and technical sections separate, so price does not anchor the technical review.

Programs that score total cost build a baseline they can reuse at renewal, instead of starting from a single bid price. Agree the cost model with finance before release, so every evaluator uses the same assumptions.

Use a single point of contact

Route all supplier communication through one named contact and publish every answer to every bidder. Side conversations between suppliers and internal stakeholders undermine fairness and create audit exposure. CIPS guidance adds that major changes to specifications must be notified to all tenderers, with an equal opportunity to amend bids.

A single contact also gives the program one record of every clarification, which matters if an award is challenged. Name a deputy for the role, so supplier questions never route around the process during absences.

Pitfalls

Common RFP Mistakes to Avoid

Each of these mistakes undermines either comparability or fairness, and each has a direct fix in the 7-step framework. For most teams, the fastest answer to how to improve RFP process outcomes is removing them.

Mistake 1

Inviting too many suppliers

Evaluation slows, scoring quality drops and serious bidders may decline to participate. Fix: qualify the field with an RFI first.

Mistake 2

Vague scope

Loose requirements produce responses that cannot be compared line by line. Fix: write measurable outcomes and specifications before drafting.

Mistake 3

Weak or unclear evaluation criteria

Criteria set after bids arrive invite bias and challenge. Fix: lock the weighted matrix before release.

Mistake 4

Skipping the feedback step

Unsuccessful suppliers learn nothing, and your team loses insight into its own process. Fix: debrief every respondent against the published criteria.

Mistake 5

Running an RFP to validate a decision already made

A predetermined outcome wastes supplier effort and damages trust. Fix: if the decision is made, document a sole-source justification instead.

Many of these mistakes start in the RFP document itself.

Supplier Responses

Writing an RFP That Gets Strong Supplier Responses

The clarity of your RFP directly determines the quality and comparability of the responses you receive. Suppliers can only answer the questions you ask, in the structure you provide.

Use specific scope language

Replace open prompts with specific, measurable requirements. State the scope, technical and functional requirements, deliverables, service levels and milestones in terms a supplier can commit to.

Open prompt

"Describe your support model" invites marketing copy.

Specific prompt

"State your committed response time for priority 1 incidents" invites a comparable answer.

Share evaluation criteria up front

Tell suppliers how they will be scored before they write a word. Published criteria and weights let suppliers focus on what matters to you, which raises the relevance of every response. Ask the same questions in the same structure, with structured fields for pricing, service levels and compliance declarations.

Allow a realistic response window

Give suppliers enough time to produce a considered proposal. A rushed window can favor incumbents and suppliers with templated answers. A realistic window, with a fixed Q&A deadline and numbered addenda, produces responses your team can actually evaluate.

Technology

How to Optimize and Automate the RFP Process

The most reliable way to optimize RFP process performance is to fix the process first, then automate the administrative work around it. Automation applied to an unstructured RFP simply moves the same inconsistencies faster.

Does automation replace human judgment?

No. Automation removes administrative drag, while the award decision stays human.

ISM published a roundtable in Inside Supply Management on May 26, 2026. In it, one procurement leader described using AI to accelerate RFP development, launch and analysis, with human oversight throughout. The leader said that oversight ensures accuracy and judgment.

FAQ

FAQ

What's the difference between an RFP and an RFQ?+
An RFP invites suppliers to propose a solution that buyers score on weighted criteria, while an RFQ compares prices for a clearly specified item. Use an RFP when the requirement is complex and an RFQ when price is the main variable.
What should you look for when evaluating an RFP response?+
Look for direct, evidence-backed answers to each published criterion, scored against anchored scales. Check that commitments are measurable and that total cost of ownership is clear. Score non-responsive or off-topic answers lower, even when the price is attractive.
Does automating the RFP process mean losing human judgment in supplier selection?+
No. Automation handles rule-based work such as routing, compliance checks and score aggregation, while evaluators still assess proposals and make the award. Well-designed tools keep human approval in the loop for every decision that matters.
How long should an RFP process take, from issue to award?+
The right duration depends on the requirement's complexity, the number of evaluators and your approval gates. Build the timeline backward from the contract start date. Give suppliers a response window that reflects the effort a credible proposal requires.
How many suppliers should you invite to respond to an RFP?+
Invite only as many qualified suppliers as your panel can evaluate thoroughly. An RFI helps you reach that shortlist by screening for capability, financial stability and compliance first. A focused, credible field tends to produce more comparable bids.
Should you give feedback to suppliers who don't win?+
Yes, CIPS describes feedback to unsuccessful tenderers as good practice. Base each debrief on the published criteria and that supplier's own scores. Feedback helps suppliers improve future bids and shows your process is fair.
What's the difference between improving the RFP process and automating it?+
Improving the RFP process fixes how decisions are made, while automating it speeds up the administrative work around those decisions. Knowing how to improve RFP process design comes first, because automation applied to a weak process repeats the same flaws faster.
What's the biggest reason RFP processes fail?+
Most RFP processes fail because of avoidable process gaps, especially vague scope and criteria set after bids arrive. Both make responses impossible to compare fairly. Clear requirements and a locked evaluation matrix prevent the most common failures.
