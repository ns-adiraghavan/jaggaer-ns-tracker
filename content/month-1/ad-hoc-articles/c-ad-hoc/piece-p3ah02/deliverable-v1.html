The Accountability Test

Why Autonomy Is the Real Test for Supply Chain AI Agents

Autonomy is the real test because accountability never transfers to the agent. We can delegate a decision to technology but can't delegate accountability. That makes an agent's limits as important as its capabilities. Large language models are probabilistic, so identical inputs can produce different outputs.

Researchers at Harvard, MIT, Purdue and Georgia Tech tested this in a supply chain simulation. Agents given identical data placed different orders from run to run. The variation widened as orders moved upstream, an effect the researchers call the agent bullwhip effect.

So, the useful comparison is not what supply chain AI agents can do. It is how each platform answers these four questions:

  • 1What gets delegated?
  • 2What triggers human review?
  • 3How is performance measured?
  • 4Who is accountable?

Platform by Platform

Where the Major Platforms Draw the Line

SAP

SAP Ariba

SAP's procurement agents combine analysis, recommendations and increasingly automated execution, while keeping humans accountable for consequential decisions. Its Bid Analysis Agent evaluates supplier bids across cost, quality and risk, explains trade-offs and recommends awards. Its Procurement Contract Assistant analyzes expiring contracts and recommends whether to renew, renegotiate, consolidate or exit. Joule's querying and summarization capabilities are currently more mature than its ability to execute complex, multi-step procurement tasks autonomously. SAP's roadmap moves toward greater autonomy, with agents operating within governed boundaries and humans remaining in the lead.

C

Coupa

Coupa is expanding agentic execution across procurement, with automation spanning payment processing and sourcing workflows. Its Payment Batch Creation Agent ran 14 batches covering 2,395 payments worth $20.1 million in five weeks, while final release remains subject to approval.

Sourcing moves faster but keeps a human checkpoint. Many customers report sourcing cycle times cut by up to 40%. Coupa is targeting fully automated flows from intake to award, with humans kept in the loop for oversight and approval.

O

Oracle

Oracle ties agent autonomy to business impact. Its agents can progress routine work within existing policies, permissions and approval hierarchies, while surfacing exceptions and decisions requiring human judgment. Oracle describes this as “human in the loop,” “human in the lead,” and autonomous execution, depending on the level of risk and governance required. Its sourcing agents can automate eligible processes through award, while approval checkpoints remain configurable.

Earned Autonomy

Supply Chain AI Agents Should Graduate to Autonomy

Think of autonomy as something agents earn over time. A new agent starts by giving advice. Once it proves reliable, it can act within set limits. Later, it may handle routine work on its own. Each step up should be backed by results, like steady savings and fewer exceptions. It should also work the other way. If an agent's performance drops, its scope should shrink.
