Definitions

What is MRO Spend in Manufacturing?

MRO spend is what a manufacturer pays for the parts, supplies and services that keep plants running but never go into the product. It covers spare parts, maintenance supplies, tools, safety gear, facility supplies and repair services. Unlike direct materials, MRO spend has no bill of materials to plan from. That changes how it is bought, and why direct and indirect procurement need different controls.

AttributeDirect MaterialsMRO Spend
Demand comes fromProduction plansWear, safety needs, breakdowns
Who buysCentral category teamsStaff at every site
Order patternLarge and scheduledSmall, frequent, often urgent
Supplier baseFew strategic suppliersMany distributors and local vendors

Each order is small, but across thousands of orders and sites, the gaps add up.

The Challenge

Why MRO and Indirect Spend are Hard to Control

MRO and indirect spend are hard to control because buying is scattered, records are inconsistent, rising prices mask waste and uptime comes first. Staff buy at every site, often mid-breakdown, when speed matters more than contracts. In a 2025 Efficio survey, 93% of European CPOs and CFOs named maverick spending a major source of leakage.

Scattered buying leaves scattered data. One bearing may carry several descriptions and supplier codes, hiding what each site pays. Rising prices then disguise leakage as inflation. Fastenal is an industrial distributor and its pricing alone added 2.4 to 2.7 points to sales growth in Q3 2025, and about 2.4 points in Q3 2026.

Tighter approvals, the usual fix, can backfire. A delayed spare part keeps a line down, and Fluke puts an average downtime up to $1.7 million per hour as per the 3-country survey. In industrial equipment procurement, spares often serve customers’ machines too, so delays spread further. Control must therefore make the compliant route the fastest route. That is the core of indirect spend management, and of lasting indirect spend savings in manufacturing.

Analysis

What to Analyze in MRO Spend

MRO spend analysis should answer 4 questions: what you buy, who you buy from, what you pay and how you buy.

DimensionWhat it reveals
What you buyDuplicate parts and near-identical specifications
Who you buy fromA long tail of small suppliers
What you payPrice gaps for the same item
How you buyOff-catalog and off-contract orders

Grouping duplicates comes first, because suppliers and prices only compare once each part is one item. As one part, the bearing filed under several descriptions shows every supplier, each site’s price and how often orders skip the catalog. Much of this evidence sits outside purchase orders. McKinsey finds procurement functions often use under 20% of their available data, with key evidence held in contracts and specifications. Full spend visibility joins purchase, invoice, contract and catalog data in one view. Doing that by hand across thousands of free-text lines does not scale.

Insight to Control

From Insight to Control: Reducing MRO Spend

Reducing MRO spend means turning each finding into a buying change: catalogs for repeat items, fewer suppliers and protection for critical spares.

Lever 1Catalogs for repeat items
Lever 2Fewer suppliers
Lever 3Protection for critical spares

Build an indirect procurement strategy around criticality

Start by sorting items by what a failure would cost. A sound indirect procurement strategy protects critical spares first, keeping stock and a backup supplier for each. Everything else can be pushed hard on price. Deloitte’s 2025 CPO survey found 74% rate active alternative sources among their most effective risk strategies.

74%

Rate active alternative sources among their most effective risk strategies

Source · Deloitte 2025 Global CPO Survey

For repeat items, the fix is the buying route. eProcurement catalogs and guided buying put the approved part, supplier and price in front of the person ordering. When the compliant route is also the fastest, technicians have little reason to buy around it. For the long supplier tail, consolidation moves volume to fewer distributors. Larger volumes earn better terms, and fewer suppliers mean fewer prices to track. Supplier risk and onboarding checks confirm the shortlist can still deliver.

McKinsey cited one company that cut MRO costs 20% by competing that volume through e-sourcing. These levers work best in a set order.

Playbook

MRO Spend Control Playbook

The best practices for MRO procurement in manufacturing follow one rule: fix the data, protect uptime, then change how people buy. Run them in this order:

  1. 1

    Unify the data. Combine ERP, p-card and invoice records, and test AI matching on last year’s orders.

    Track: share of spend classified.

  2. 2

    Rank by criticality. Agree with maintenance which spares can stop a line.

    Track: critical spares with a backup source.

  3. 3

    Close the biggest gaps. Merge top duplicates and align prices across sites.

    Track: price variance on top items.

  4. 4

    Catalog repeat buys. Make agreed items the quickest to order.

    Track: catalog share of orders.

  5. 5

    Consolidate suppliers. Move tail volume to a vetted shortlist.

    Track: active MRO suppliers.

  6. 6

    Review yearly. New parts, sites and suppliers reopen old leaks. Only 19% of firms in Efficio’s 2025 survey review indirect spend annually.

    Track: new leakage found.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is MRO spend analysis?

+

MRO spend analysis reviews maintenance, repair and operations purchases to show what a manufacturer buys, from which suppliers and at what price. It exposes duplicate parts, price gaps between sites and off-contract buying.

What counts as MRO spend in manufacturing?

+

MRO spend in manufacturing covers spare parts, maintenance supplies, tools, safety gear, facility supplies and repair services. These purchases keep plants running but never become part of the finished product.

What is an indirect procurement strategy for MRO?

+

An indirect procurement strategy for MRO sorts items by how critical they are to production. Critical spares get assured supply, while commodity items are bought through catalogs at negotiated prices.

How does AI improve MRO spend analysis?

+

AI improves MRO spend analysis by classifying free-text purchase lines, merging duplicate parts and flagging price outliers automatically. Category managers then review the findings instead of cleaning spreadsheets by hand.

How can manufacturers reduce MRO spend?

+

Manufacturers reduce MRO spend by routing repeat items to eProcurement catalogs, consolidating suppliers and standardizing duplicate parts. Critical spares should keep a backup supplier so cost cuts never risk uptime.
