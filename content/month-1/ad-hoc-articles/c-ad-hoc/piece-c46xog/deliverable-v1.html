Off-Contract Buying in Higher Education: How Guided Buying Helps

By Visnja Radosavljevic · Published: November 16, 2026 · 12 min read

Departments buy off contract when the approved route is slower than the workaround. Higher education guided buying closes that gap. It puts contracted suppliers and negotiated pricing in front of the buyer before the order exists. You can keep the discounts you negotiated without a campus mandate to defend.

Guided buying matters most where buying is decentralized by design: colleges, departments and research units that purchase with their own autonomy. It matters just as much for community college procurement. This post looks at college procurement optimization through off-contract buying in higher education and the off-contract spending it creates. It covers why it persists, where it happens, what it costs, how it touches compliance and what guided buying changes. Figures and rules are current as of November 2026.

Why departments buy off contract

Departments buy off contract when the approved route costs more time than the workaround, and delay is a common complaint. In a 2026 survey of 50 US university procurement staff, 62% named delayed approvals among their top 3 challenges (Inside Higher Ed, March 2026). THE Consultancy produced the paper with support from Amazon Business, a business purchasing marketplace. In the same survey, 72% reported some duplicated purchasing effort.

Much of the cause is design, not defiance. The Hackett Group, an advisory firm, analyzed intake in March 2026. Poor intake, it says, forces users to learn procurement’s structure before they can get work done. Picture a lab waiting on consumables: it will not read the contract portfolio. It will order from the supplier it already knows.

Calendar pressure adds to it. Respondents to the 50-staff survey said end-of-year deadlines and carryover rules manufacture urgency. Rules that differ between departments set off a rush to complete contracts before the fiscal year closes. These 4 triggers recur:

Urgency. A deadline or an unplanned need sends the buyer to the fastest checkout.
Familiarity. Buyers return to the suppliers they already use, contract or not.
Awareness. Buyers cannot use a contract they do not know exists.
Coverage. If an item is not in a catalog, the contract cannot win the order.

E&I Cooperative Services, a purchasing cooperative, lists the causes of off-contract spending in its June 2026 analysis. They include urgent need, lack of awareness of approved contracts, supplier preference and frustration with inefficient purchasing. Its remedies start with making contracted purchasing easier: more awareness, simpler workflows, user-friendly catalogs and training. A mandate tells buyers what to do and leaves the faster path in place.

Where off-contract buying happens

Off-contract buying clusters where purchasing is decentralized, as E&I’s June 2026 analysis describes for higher education. In the 50-staff survey, 25% of staff said purchasing decisions at their university are mostly or fully decentralized. Only half use a dedicated e-procurement platform.

Separately, 82% said they handle at least some transactions manually or outside established systems. That is 41 of the 50 respondents. It does not measure what share of spend is manual. It does show that most of the procurement staff surveyed work partly outside one workflow.

Add up single orders across many departments and the negotiated rate on paper can drift away from the price actually paid.

Marketplaces belong in this picture. At Tulsa Community College, Amazon was a small share of the workload. It took about half the purchasing team’s time, according to Assistant Director of Purchasing Austin Rosenthal. The college’s Jaggaer case study attributes that to marketplaces such as Amazon and Best Buy running as silos, disconnected from the rest of the system.

Tail spend is a different measure. It describes how small and scattered purchases are. E&I defines it as the large number of low-value purchases outside primary spending categories, often spread across departments. Off-contract describes whether a contract was used. A small purchase can be both. For more on higher education tail spend, see our guide to tail spend management.

E&I names 3 example categories to target. Facilities and maintenance, repair and operations (MRO) carry high lifecycle costs. IT hardware and software licensing often leaves redundant campus subscriptions unmanaged. Janitorial, sanitation and office supplies are frequently bought off contract at higher than contract rates (October 2026).

What off-contract buying costs a university

Off-contract buying can cost the discount you negotiated plus the staff time spent clearing exceptions. The August 2026 review found no independently verified, current rate of maverick spend in higher education, so measure your own. The cost lands on thin margins. NACUBO’s 2026 survey of 620 business officers and other professionals ranks managing rising costs first. It notes that Moody’s, S&P and Fitch each hold a negative sector outlook. Thin margins, it says, leave little room to absorb a wrong assumption.

Business officers feel that pressure, but few say they have the cost detail to act on it. Inside Higher Ed and Hanover Research surveyed chief business officers in 2026, with support from TIAA and Strata Decision Technology. Of 213 respondents (a 9% response rate, margin of error 6%), 62% say their institution is already streamlining administrative processes to cut overhead. Only 13% say it understands per-student costs by program very well. Structural cost imbalance, costs growing faster than revenue, ranks as the second-biggest 5-year financial risk at 42%, behind enrollment at 46%.

Process cost is the second bill. APQC, a nonprofit benchmarking organization, put the average cost of processing a purchase order (PO) at $42 in its April 2026 benchmarks. The cost runs from about $14 at the 25th percentile to $54.50 at the 75th. At 100,000 POs a year, top performers spend roughly $1.38 million against $5.45 million for higher-cost peers. APQC names purchasing governance, including limiting off-contract buying, as 1 of 3 practices that separate top performers from their peers. These are cross-industry benchmarks, not higher education figures.

The benefit at stake varies by contract. E&I says its Economic Benefit Model typically delivers 5% to 12% or more in total economic benefit, counting cost reduction, cost avoidance and incentives together. That is not a price discount alone. Actual results depend on category, current pricing, volume and contract adoption (October 2026). E&I’s own guidance adds that projected savings should be adjusted when departments buy off contract.

Here is the arithmetic, as an illustration. APQC’s open benchmark puts the median share of total purchases bought through maverick buying, where an existing purchasing contract is ignored, at 1.2%. The sample is 6,182 companies across all industries (accessed November 17, 2026). On a hypothetical $100 million in total purchases, that is $1.2 million off contract. At E&I’s reported 5% to 12%, the forgone total economic benefit on that $1.2 million would be $60,000 to $144,000 a year. The inputs come from different populations, so swap in your own off-contract rate and your own contract benefit.

Off-contract buying and compliance

Off-contract purchases can weaken the audit trail on federally funded work, because proof of competition and reasonable pricing lives inside the procurement workflow. As of November 2026, the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) sets the micro-purchase threshold at $15,000. It sets the simplified acquisition threshold at $350,000. Both took effect October 1, 2025 (Federal Register).

The Uniform Guidance, issued by the Office of Management and Budget (OMB), treats those thresholds as the baseline for federal awards. It is Part 200 of Title 2 of the Code of Federal Regulations (2 CFR). Our OMB Uniform Guidance procurement rules analysis explains the details.

The federal lines at a glance, for purchases charged to federal awards:

Purchase sizeFederal methodWhat it takes
Up to $15,000Micro-purchaseNo competitive quotes if the price is reasonable and documented
Above $15,000, up to $350,000Simplified acquisitionPrice or rate quotations from an adequate number of qualified sources
Above $350,000Formal competitionSealed bids or competitive proposals, depending on the procurement

Your institution documents its own micro-purchase threshold. It can set the threshold lower than the FAR’s, or self-certify a threshold up to $50,000 each year with a justification and supporting documentation. A threshold above $50,000 needs approval from the cognizant agency for indirect costs (2 CFR 200.320).

A recipient can award a micro-purchase without competitive quotes if it considers the price reasonable. That judgment rests on research, experience, purchase history or other information, and the recipient keeps documents to support it. To the extent practicable, recipients should also distribute micro-purchases equitably among qualified suppliers.

Both thresholds apply to the aggregate amount of the procurement transaction (2 CFR 200.320). Splitting one need into several orders to stay under a threshold does not change which method applies.

Awareness of the rules is a gap. In the 50-staff survey, 26% of staff said they do not know whether external policies affect procurement at all.

The rulebook is still moving. OMB proposed a rewrite of 2 CFR Part 200 on May 29, 2026. It is not final. Congress has barred OMB from finalizing it through December 11, 2026, according to the Congressional Research Service. The current rules stay in force for now, and OMB could finalize after December 11. Our grant procurement compliance checklist tracks what to watch.

The practical fix puts the check where the buyer already is, at requisition. That keeps documentation inside the buying workflow, with no separate documentation project. The grant subsection below shows how.

Frequently asked questions

What is higher education guided buying?+

Higher education guided buying is a purchasing workflow that steers campus buyers to contracted suppliers and approved catalogs before an order exists. In Jaggaer eProcurement it enforces contracted suppliers at search, routes non-catalog requests through intake and applies negotiated pricing in the cart. Jaggaer flags and tracks off-contract exceptions.

What is off-contract buying in higher education?+

Off-contract buying is any purchase made outside a negotiated agreement or approved supplier list, usually by a department, college or research unit. Maverick spend is the closely related term. It implies a stronger element of policy bypass, though many teams use both terms interchangeably.

How much off-contract spend is normal at a university?+

Jaggaer’s August 2026 review found no independently verified, current higher education rate. The nonprofit benchmarking organization APQC reports that a median of 1.2% of total purchases are bought through maverick buying, across 6,182 companies of all industries. Treat that as a reference point, not a target. The reliable number is your own, from comparing contract rates with what departments actually paid.

Does guided buying force departments to use specific suppliers?+

It enforces contracted suppliers at search, and your intake workflows decide what happens to everything else. Departments still shop, including marketplaces behind punchout.

Do cooperative contracts count as on-contract spend?+

That depends on your state and local rules and your own policy. E&I Cooperative Services competitively solicits its cooperative agreements. Only your procurement office can confirm that one satisfies your competition requirements. Then load the agreement into the catalog so buyers can find it.

What is a group purchasing organization in education?+

A group purchasing organization (GPO), or cooperative, negotiates contracts on behalf of member institutions, using collective buying power. E&I Cooperative Services, for example, serves more than 6,500 education institutions as of October 2026. Savings from a cooperative contract reach your budget when departments buy against it.
