What is agentic AI in source to pay?

Agentic AI in source to pay is AI that carries out procurement tasks instead of only answering questions. Typical tasks include checking a request against policy, building a sourcing event and flagging a duplicate invoice. Because these tasks run inside live workflows, every agent needs defined permissions, policy controls, traceable outputs and a named human decision-maker.

What does governance look like when AI Workers do the work?

Agentic AI governance becomes visible in how AI Workers behave. Depending on the role, an AI Worker checks requests against policy, respects user permissions, cites source passages, raises alerts or flags mismatches. The AI Worker recommends or prepares the work. A named person makes the decision, and JAI guardrails define where the AI Worker stops.

What does ISO/IEC 42001 mean for procurement AI?

ISO/IEC 42001 is the first international standard for AI management systems, according to ISO. For procurement AI, the standard gives buyers a reference point for how a vendor manages AI policy, risk and oversight roles. Jaggaer says it was the first source to pay vendor certified to ISO/IEC 42001 for AI management.

Certification applies to the management system, not to individual AI outputs. For teams reviewing ISO 42001 mandatory controls for AI risk management, ISO’s guidance sets 2 boundaries. Certification is voluntary, and the standard does not replace laws or regulations. No certificate guarantees regulatory compliance or error-free outcomes. Day-to-day governance still depends on policy checks, user permissions and human review.

Readers who want more detail can explore the JAI datasheet and its Trusted AI framework.

Do buyers still make the final decision with AI Workers?

Yes. In each of the 6 AI Worker roles Jaggaer describes, a named person keeps the final decision. The JAI Sourcing Advisor recommends an award, and the buyer decides. The JAI Supplier Assistant recommends approve, correct or escalate, and the reviewer decides. AI Workers handle preparation and checks, while procurement professionals stay accountable for outcomes.

Agentic AI governance depends on keeping execution, recommendation and accountability separate. Each layer has a distinct role:

  • Execution: Groundwork such as filled forms, built events and raised alerts.
  • Recommendation: A proposed outcome, such as a supplier award or an onboarding action.
  • Accountability: The decision itself, held by a named person.
