Intake to Procure vs Procure to Pay: What Is the Difference?

8 min read | By Visnja Radosavljevic | Published November 2026

Intake to procure decides which procurement path a business need takes, and procure to pay carries the purchase from requisition to supplier payment. Both processes overlap at the requisition, and Jaggaer runs them on one platform, so controls set at the request stage can carry through to payment.

Most of the confusion in intake to procure vs procure to pay starts at that overlap. A requisition sits in an approval queue, or an invoice arrives without its purchase order (PO), and nobody knows which process owns the problem.

This guide explains the difference between intake to procure and procure to pay, including where each one starts and stops.

What is intake to procure?

Intake to procure runs from the first ask to an approved purchase order. It captures the request and checks it against policy and budget before it reaches the right reviewers. Some requests route to a sourcing event first.

Say a department needs a batch of laptops. Intake decides whether that is a catalog order or something that needs a sourcing event, and who has to approve it. The need comes first. The requisition is the formal request to buy it.

What is procure to pay?

Procure to pay (P2P) is the purchasing and payment process. It starts at the purchase requisition and runs through the PO, goods receipt, invoice matching and supplier payment. In Jaggaer the requisition step, including guided buying and catalogs, sits inside eProcurement.

Where three-way matching applies, the invoice has to match both the PO and the goods receipt, and mismatches go to review before anyone pays.

How they fit together: from intake to payment

Procurement intake to payment is a chain of handoffs. Intake decides where a need goes, and procure to pay buys the goods and pays the supplier.

Follow one need, the laptops, through the process.

1

Intake captures and reviews it.

2

Someone creates and approves a requisition, and purchasing issues a PO.

3

Then the goods arrive, AP matches the invoice and the supplier gets paid.

Intake owns the first steps and procure to pay owns the last, and both processes share the requisition and the PO. Some non-PO invoices are legitimate. Others can trace back to a purchase that skipped intake.

Do you need both?

Many organizations with complex purchasing end up wanting both, though not always in separate tools.

If requests come in by email and chat and maverick spend keeps showing up, start with intake.
If requests arrive clean but invoices stall at matching, start with procure to pay.

Where source to pay and orchestration fit

Source to pay adds sourcing and contracting upstream of procure to pay. Source to contract is that upstream half. The guide to source to contract vs source to pay vs procure to pay compares them in detail.

Orchestration coordinates the people and systems a request touches. In Jaggaer’s framing, intake captures and classifies the request and orchestration coordinates the steps to fulfill it. Together they can trigger downstream steps such as supplier onboarding, contract management or PO creation. A new software subscription, say, might need IT and legal to review it in parallel before anyone raises a requisition.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between intake to procure and procure to pay? +
Intake to procure runs from the first ask to an approved purchase order, and some requests route to a sourcing event first. Procure to pay starts at the requisition and runs through receiving, invoice matching and payment. They share the requisition and PO stages, and boundaries vary by platform.
Which comes first, the purchase order (PO) or the purchase requisition (PR)? +
The purchase requisition comes first. Once approved, it can generate the PO that goes to the supplier. Some purchases follow other workflows.
What is the difference between procure to pay (P2P) and source to pay (S2P)? +
Procure to pay covers purchasing, receiving, invoice processing and supplier payment. Source to pay adds sourcing and contracting before all of that.
Do I need intake to procure if I already have procure to pay? +
If your P2P workflows already collect the right information and send purchases to the right approvers, you may not need a separate intake tool. Organizations with complex requests or scattered submission channels may benefit from structured intake that connects to P2P.
