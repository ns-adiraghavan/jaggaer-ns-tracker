Change Management in Procurement: 10 Principles for Success

About 15 min read | By Visnja Radosavljevic | Published May 2024 | Updated October 2026

Change management in procurement is a structured approach to preparing people for new processes, technology and ways of working. It covers the people, process and technology changes a procurement organization takes on and the work of getting each one adopted. In Hackett’s 2026 Procurement Key Issues Study, 43% of procurement organizations are actively pursuing AI deployment. That is nearly double last year’s level.

Change can create friction when it alters established roles, controls or daily work. How you respond to that friction, however, determines the rest of the story. Each company, team, and corporate culture is unique, which is why all organizations approach change differently.

Digital transformation is among the biggest changes an organization faces, and success is still the exception. In McKinsey’s 2021 survey, less than one-third of respondents said their companies’ transformations succeeded at both improving performance and sustaining the gains. Your approach to procurement change management is critical.

AI has raised the stakes. In McKinsey’s 2026 survey of 1,719 respondents across industries, nearly 9 in 10 report regular AI use in at least one business function. At the same time, 37% attribute at least some impact on earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT) to AI. That is about the same share as last year. McKinsey’s report authors write: “The limiting factor is increasingly the organization’s ability to absorb change.” Absorbing that change is what procurement change management is for.

What Is Change Management in Procurement?

It is both a strategy and a philosophy. Leadership has to embrace it, and the entire organization has to enact it to achieve the desired outcome.

The Golden Triangle

The Golden Triangle framework (people, process, technology) is essential for effective change management in digital transformation. People should always be the priority, which boosts digital adoption and, ultimately, creates smoother organizational change.

Jaggaer’s Adopt datasheet (2022) makes the same point from the product side. It states that continuous return on investment depends heavily on successful change management.

Principles for Successful Procurement Change Management

Procurement change management rests on 10 principles: be realistic, start at the top, be thorough, set a roadmap and create ownership. The others are to communicate constantly, assess the landscape, address it, prepare to fail and remember the individuals. Your internal culture will shape your strategy, but these 10 principles can start the conversation.

1. Be Realistic

Some company cultures struggle with change, and some even resist it outright. You need to look at the company’s history, culture and leadership to set realistic expectations.

Some companies are more agile than others, some thrive in chaos while others need a rigid schedule. Understand that and set your expectations accordingly. Rome wasn’t built in a day and your company won’t transform overnight.

Timing is part of realism, and so are targets. Danish Crown’s transformation ran in 6 phases from 2019 to mid-2023, after it chose Jaggaer in 2018. Respondents to McKinsey’s 2021 survey say nearly a quarter of a transformation’s value loss occurs at target-setting. They say 55% occurs during and after implementation. McKinsey’s 2023 survey points the same way. Respondents report value lost across all 4 phases, most notably during execution. So set targets you can sustain after go-live.

2. Start at the Top

Leaders must model the change before they ask anyone else to make it.

A common pattern starts at a companywide meeting, where the CEO announces changes to the company or its culture.

Fast forward 6 months and nothing has changed, because change has to start at the top.

Leaders and managers must set an example first and show people what change looks like. Expecting people to follow blind orders won’t get anyone very far.

The data points the same way. McKinsey’s 2026 survey classes about 6% of respondents as AI high performers. Its exhibits count 92 of them. They attribute at least 5% of EBIT to AI and describe its impact as significant. They are twice as likely as other respondents to say senior leaders demonstrate commitment to AI initiatives. It is a self-reported finding from many industries. It fits what leaders already see: commitment shows in what they do, not in what they announce.

3. Be Thorough

Don’t silo change. Especially with digital transformation, the change will affect all layers of the company, from top leadership to interns.

Procurement leaders already name the obstacles. In Deloitte’s 2025 Global CPO Survey of 250+ chief procurement officers (CPOs) across 40 countries, respondents listed barriers to procurement value delivery. Each figure is the share of CPOs who put that barrier in their top 3. Siloed ways of working came first at 57%. Competing priorities followed at 46%. Organizational or technology capability to support execution reached 40%. The talent gap reached 34%. Deloitte adds that putting technology like generative AI (GenAI) in place is no small feat. It cites fragmented technology, organizational silos, poor data and change management hurdles. A plan scoped to one team leaves barriers like these in place. If suppliers will submit catalogs, invoices or bids through the new process, put them in the roadmap too.

4. Create a Roadmap/Clear Vision

State why the change is needed and show the roadmap, so people can see the value.

Change is hard. People will naturally be resistant to it because that’s human nature.

Have a clear vision statement that explains what the benefits will be and exactly why change is needed. When people understand the value of change and can see the roadmap, that’s when real change happens.

McKinsey’s 2023 transformation survey (n=472) found it critical that employees understand why they are being asked to change. Respondents who said their transformations fostered understanding and conviction were 3.2 times as likely to report outperforming their peers after the transformation began. The comparison is with respondents whose transformations lacked that action. The result is self-reported, so it points in a direction without proving it.

5. Create Ownership

Company buy-in is great, but change happens when leaders take charge. There need to be project owners for all the various moving parts.

If something isn’t tracking right someone needs to handle adjusting course. Likewise, if something is running smoothly, recognize it.

Ownership includes owning the measures. In McKinsey’s 2026 survey, the same AI high performers are twice as likely as others to report defined processes for measuring AI impact. The group is small and self-reported, and the finding is specific to AI, so it hints at a link without proving one.

Measure adoption and compliance separately. Usage data shows who is using the system and where they stall. Purchase order compliance shows whether spend follows policy. The two can change separately. Track adoption by role, policy compliance and exception rates from go-live, and report them separately. 6. You Can’t Overcommunicate

Communicate more often than feels necessary, face to face where you can. Repeat the message at every stage of the change, not only at launch.

Communication is central to creating employee buy-in, digital adoption, and ultimately digital transformation.

McKinsey’s 2021 survey adds detail on how. Respondents whose transformations were successful were more likely to use face-to-face communication to make goals tangible, and 65% of them cited line-manager briefings.

7. Assess the Current Landscape

Map your culture, processes and existing solutions before you change anything.

You need a full understanding of what your transformation means, but also who it affects, and what you need to change. This is where procurement process redesign starts. Map each process as it runs today, not as the policy describes it, and note where users work around it. Usage data makes that concrete. According to its 2022 datasheet, Jaggaer’s Adopt provides usage analytics that show how individuals and teams use, or do not use, a solution’s capabilities. It also helps identify process bottlenecks.

8. Address the Current Landscape

Once you’ve gotten your overview, redesign the processes, training and capabilities it flags.

Work with your leaders to create new processes or training guides, eliminate overlapping capabilities and fill in gaps. Put training guides where users already work, which is what in-app user training provides. Jaggaer’s Adopt datasheet (2022) lists identifying focal points for change management and process redesign among its benefits.

The data points to redesign as well as rollout. In McKinsey’s 2026 survey, nearly three-quarters of the AI high performers report fundamentally redesigning workflows because of AI. That is up from 55% a year earlier. About one-quarter of other respondents say the same. The finding comes from many industries and is specific to AI. Procurement process redesign is most important where a tool changes decision rights, handoffs or controls. In those cases, redesign the affected process instead of only moving the old one into software. Deloitte’s 2025 CPO survey makes the case from the procurement side. CPOs need to work with other functions and suppliers to “ruthlessly simplify and automate lower-value processes.” They also need to make sure people have the skills for higher-value work.

9. Prepare to Fail

No matter how good or well thought out a plan is, parts of it will fall short.

People react in different ways, market conditions change, maybe your processes aren’t working out like you thought they would. That’s normal. Prioritize problems by adoption risk and operational impact, and fix them in controlled steps.

In its Jaggaer customer story (2023), Joanna Hryneńko-Dudek, head of Danish Crown’s procurement center of excellence, describes user pushback. The team paused the rollout, explained the goals and asked users for their own vision. It then adjusted the plan so that not everything happened at once. Her advice to other leaders: “Few people like change so take nothing for granted!”

10. Remember the Individuals

People are the key. Do everything with people in mind. Train them, develop skills, make processes to suit your people’s needs, and then clearly communicate at all stages.

Nothing we’ve talked about up to this point can work without people. In McKinsey’s 2023 survey, respondents whose organizations gave people tools to apply new skills were 3.8 times as likely to report outperforming peers. Those tools included dashboards and project plans.

Training has to fit how people actually work. At Virginia Commonwealth University, adjunct professors and student staff used the system only a few times a year. According to its 2024 case study, VCU replaced a 300-page manual and classroom sessions with Adopt Premium guides. The rollout took a few weeks and has produced 15 guides to date. The team reports fewer errors and faster requisitions.

Procurement data shows the readiness gap. In Deloitte’s 2025 CPO survey, only 4% of CPOs have complete confidence in their team’s ability to execute. Another 49% have confidence to a large extent. The gap is wide on AI understanding. Deloitte’s label “Digital Masters” covers top-quartile performers with top-quartile capabilities. Of those, 43% rate their procurement organization’s understanding of AI as good or extensive. For Followers, the figure is 7%.

Change Management for eProcurement Adoption

The core of change management for eProcurement adoption is to make the compliant way to buy the easiest way to buy. Jaggaer’s eProcurement software starts from that principle. Jaggaer states that guided buying enforces contracted suppliers at search and routes non-catalog requests through configurable intake workflows. Deloitte’s 2025 CPO survey points the same way. It says many CPOs are focusing on intake and triage. That signals a need to build automated guidance, through process orchestration, toward preferred sourcing, purchasing and paying paths and suppliers.

Danish Crown took the same approach. It set out to build a user-friendly buying environment to ensure adoption, using guided buying, managed supplier catalogs and configurable purchase request forms. According to its 2023 customer story, more than 60% of its purchases were purchase order-compliant in the new systems at the time. The target was 80%. That figure measures compliant purchases, not user adoption rates. Danish Crown also reports that adoption and performance were extremely high in its most recent business-unit eProcurement implementation.

The 10 principles apply directly to change management in eProcurement. Assess how people buy today (Principle 7). Remove the workarounds that bypass the system (Principle 8). Train people inside the application rather than around it (Principle 10).

Change Management in a Procurement Transformation Program

Procurement transformation change management starts with sequencing: phase the program, as Danish Crown did by going live in phases, starting with source-to-contract.

Digital transformation involves organizational changes on multiple levels. It affects stakeholders across departments and requires cross-functional collaboration from senior leadership to the bottom rung. Beyond adopting a new solution, digital transformation is a combination of digital solutions that allows your organization to function differently. As a result of digital transformation, job functions and processes can change. That kind of change causes friction, and that’s where change management comes in.

Danish Crown’s phasing is specific. According to its customer story (2023), source-to-contract went live first, in December 2019, covering project management, sourcing, contract lifecycle management and supplier value management. Procure-to-pay followed step by step. So far, 2 major category areas are in scope: maintenance, repair and operations (MRO) and spare parts, and all indirect spend. Categories that involve contact with food, where hygiene, safety and quality requirements are very strict, get extra care before automation. Hryneńko-Dudek says users in source-to-contract, where the rollout began, are strategically oriented and ready to embrace change.

Readiness comes before each phase. The same story advises getting your data in order and clarifying your scope before you start automating. It also advises weighing the maturity and culture of each business unit, because different units move at different speeds.

Procurement process redesign belongs in the same sequence. Danish Crown lists standardization and simplification of business processes, alongside automation, among its reasons for transforming. Decide what each process should look like before you configure it in your source-to-pay platform. Then the platform carries the new process rather than the old one.

Covering every element also counts. McKinsey’s 2023 survey groups transformation actions into 4 elements. Will is the organization’s commitment to the aspiration. Skill is the capabilities it needs to succeed. Rigor is the performance infrastructure behind disciplined execution. Scope is the range of outcomes the transformation aims to improve. Few organizations get there. Only 6% of respondents reported transformations that used all 4 elements. Among respondents whose transformations used all 4, 39% say they outperformed peers. Among those that used just one, 16% say so. Both results are self-reported.

FAQ

What is change management in procurement? +
It is the planned work of moving buyers, approvers, suppliers and leaders from one way of buying to another. It covers the people, process and technology sides of a change, with people first, and its aim is adoption that lasts after go-live.
How do you manage procurement change? +
Cover the 4 elements McKinsey’s 2023 survey links to outperformance (will, skill, rigor and scope) and expect to adjust as you go. Danish Crown paused its rollout when users pushed back and reset the plan. Its advice, from its customer story, is to ask people’s opinions, monitor satisfaction and point out the gains they will see.
How do you drive eProcurement user adoption? +
Start by making the approved route the simplest one: contracted suppliers first, and a defined path for anything outside the catalog. Then support users inside the application, and watch usage data to see who is not using what.
What are the steps of the change management process? +
There is no single standard sequence. The frameworks teams cite most include Kotter’s 8-step process for leading change and its enhanced version, the 8 Accelerators. They also include the Prosci ADKAR model. ADKAR tracks individuals through 5 outcomes: awareness, desire, knowledge, ability and reinforcement. The 10 principles above overlap with themes in both, adapted to procurement, from setting realistic expectations to planning for setbacks.
