Maverick Spend: Definition, Examples and 5 Tips to Prevent and Control It

12 min read | Published: September 2020 | Updated: November 2026 | By Visnja Radosavljevic

Maverick spend is any purchase made outside an organization’s agreed procurement process: off contract, from a non-approved supplier or without the required approval. Each off-contract purchase chips away at negotiated savings, supplier risk checks and the reliability of spend data.

According to a September 2026 survey by Agiloft, a contract software vendor, procurement leaders estimate that at least 15% of supplier spend flows outside contracted agreements. The survey covered 2,005 senior procurement leaders in the US and UK. Of those leaders, 40% put the off-contract share between 16% and 30%. At that scale, off-contract buying stops being a compliance footnote. It becomes a margin issue the CFO will want explained.

This article covers what maverick spending is, why it happens and what it costs. It then sets out 5 practical steps to find off-contract buying and prevent it from returning. Each step builds on the spend management best practices procurement teams already apply to contracted categories.

What is maverick spend?

Maverick spend is spending that bypasses the purchasing rules an organization has agreed. In practice, maverick spend takes 5 forms:

  • buying from non-approved suppliers
  • buying outside contract terms from approved suppliers
  • skipping requisition or approval steps
  • raising a purchase order after the invoice arrives
  • splitting orders to stay under approval thresholds

The common thread is simple: procurement had no opportunity to influence the purchase. You will also hear the same behavior called rogue spend or off-contract spend.

Maverick spend is not the same as tail spend or fraud. Tail spend is low-value, fragmented buying and can be fully compliant. Fraud involves intent to deceive and needs its own investigation path.

What maverick spending looks like in practice

Most procurement teams will recognize these scenarios:

  • The card-funded subscription: A marketing manager buys an annual SaaS subscription on a corporate card, while IT already licenses a similar tool.
  • The after-the-fact PO: A department books an event venue without a PO. Finance raises the PO once the venue’s invoice arrives.
  • The out-of-scope order: A buyer orders from a preferred supplier but selects items the contract does not cover, at list price.
  • The split order: A team breaks one purchase into several smaller orders to stay under an approval threshold.
  • The false saving (illustrative): An engineer negotiates a discount on a part by phone, outside the eProcurement catalog. The manual processing the off-catalog order triggers can cost more than the discount saves.

None of these buyers set out to break policy. That observation points straight to the causes.

Common causes of maverick spending

Maverick spending is usually a process problem, not a people problem. Employees go around procurement when the approved route is slow, unclear or missing the item they need. Making the compliant route faster changes buying behavior more reliably than enforcement alone.

In practice, 6 drivers explain much off-contract buying:

  • Friction: Long approval chains and complex requisition forms push urgent requests outside the system.
  • Catalog and contract gaps: When an item is missing from the catalog, requesters find their own supplier.
  • Low policy awareness: Many employees do not know which suppliers hold contracts or which thresholds apply.
  • Decentralized card spend: P-cards and local budgets make off-policy purchases easy to make and hard to see.
  • Software and services buying: SaaS subscriptions and service engagements often start on a card or a signed quote before procurement is involved.
  • No feedback loop: Budget owners rarely see the off-contract share of their own spend, so buying habits stay the same.

Leaders rate software as the riskiest category for cost leakage. In Efficio’s October 2025 survey of large European companies, 81% of CPOs and CFOs named IT and software, and 68% named professional services.

Each of these drivers carries a cost, and that cost reaches well beyond a missed discount.

Why maverick spend matters

Maverick spend erodes value that procurement has already negotiated. Every off-contract purchase gives up a negotiated rate and weakens the volume commitments behind it. Procurement then walks into the next renewal with less leverage.

Ardent Partners reports an average first-year savings opportunity of 6–12% for every dollar of spend brought under procurement’s control. Lost savings are only the most visible cost.

Beyond lost savings, 2 other costs are easier to miss:

  • Supplier risk: Unvetted suppliers skip checks on financial health, data security, insurance and regulatory compliance.
  • Unreliable data: Off-contract spend distorts category baselines, forecasts and sourcing decisions.

The benefits of reducing maverick spend extend well beyond cost avoidance. Negotiated savings reach the P&L, and spend data becomes reliable enough to plan from. Risk checks then cover every supplier the organization actually pays. Consolidated volume also gives category managers a stronger hand at renewal.

Most maverick spend reduction benefits compound over time, because cleaner spend data sharpens every sourcing event that follows.

Maverick spend statistics (2025–2026)

Efficio’s October 2025 survey found that 93% of CPOs and CFOs cite maverick spending as a major contributor to cost leakage. The 300 respondents work at large European companies in the UK, Germany, the Nordics and France, each with sales of £1 billion or more. The visibility gap is just as stark: in the same survey, 85% of leaders say more than a quarter of their indirect spend has no financial oversight.

Ardent Partners' benchmark research shows how much control is achievable. Ardent’s top 20% of performers often manage more than 90% of enterprise spend, while the remaining organizations sometimes manage less than 60%.

Use these maverick spend statistics as reference points when you set targets in Tip 5. The data makes the scale clear. The next question is what procurement teams can do about it.

5 tips to prevent and control maverick spend

Reducing maverick spend works as a sequence, with each step feeding the next. Detection shows where the leaks are. Process changes remove the reasons for them. Ownership and measurement stop them from coming back.

The 5 maverick spend reduction tips below show how to reduce maverick spend in procurement without adding approval layers that simply create new workarounds.

Tip 1: Identify maverick spend through spend analysis

Step 1 of 5 · Detect

Start with a maverick spend analysis across every payment channel, because off-contract buying hides in the channels procurement does not review. Consolidate AP, P-card, T&E and ERP data into one classified view. Spend analytics tools automate the classification. For a primer on the method, see what is spend analysis.

Once the data is in one place, look for 5 patterns:

  • invoices without a PO, or with a PO dated after the invoice
  • non-preferred suppliers in contracted categories
  • invoiced prices above contracted rates from approved suppliers
  • orders split just below approval thresholds
  • recurring card charges from software and service vendors

Track one headline metric: the maverick spend rate. Divide off-policy spend by addressable spend, then multiply by 100.

Worked example (illustrative figures): Addressable spend is $40 million. The spend analysis classifies $6 million as off-policy. $6M ÷ $40M × 100 = 15% maverick spend rate.

Report the rate by category, business unit and supplier. That level of detail gives procurement the spend visibility to go after the largest leaks first.

Detection only pays off when it leads to root-cause analysis. In the engineer scenario above, the real problem was the catalog price, not the engineer. An in-system quote request removes the engineer’s reason to phone the supplier directly.

Tip 2: Make compliant purchasing easier with guided buying

Step 2 of 5 · Remove friction

Make the compliant buying route the fastest route, and most requesters will take the compliant route. Guided buying steers each request to the right channel at the moment of need. Catalogs, punch-outs and contracted items appear first, before requesters go looking elsewhere.

Start with 4 practical moves:

  • Rank contracted items and preferred suppliers at the top of search results.
  • Add punch-out catalogs for high-volume categories such as MRO, office and lab supplies.
  • Offer an in-system quote request for items outside the catalog.
  • Pre-fill cost centers, GL codes and approvers to reduce form time.

eProcurement software with guided buying applies purchasing rules when the request is made, not after the invoice arrives.

Quick win this week: Add the most frequent off-catalog items from the Tip 1 analysis to the catalog or a punch-out.

Tip 3: Review and simplify the entire procurement process

Step 3 of 5 · Redesign

Map the full request-to-pay journey, then remove every step that does not reduce risk. Start by measuring the time from a requester’s need to an approved PO. Multi-day cycles for low-risk items create the friction behind most workarounds.

That friction calls for 4 process changes:

  • One front door: Route every request through a single intake point, so requesters do not need to know which form, policy or team applies. Intake management software triages each request to the right path.
  • Risk-based approvals: Tier approvals by value and risk, instead of sending every request through the same approval chain.
  • Controlled supplier onboarding: Define who can add a new supplier and which checks apply.
  • “No PO, no pay”: Enforce the rule in AP once the compliant route is easy to use.

Tip 4: Establish clear accountability and purchasing policies

Step 4 of 5 · Assign ownership

Procurement owns the purchasing process, but budget owners own the spend. Every manager should know what the team spends, with which suppliers, and how much of that spend falls outside contract. Without that visibility, accountability stays theoretical.

Make accountability concrete in 4 ways:

  • Keep the purchasing policy to one page covering thresholds, approved channels and the exception process.
  • Set P-card limits by merchant category and value, and review card spend monthly.
  • Send each budget owner a monthly report of off-contract spend and the maverick spend rate.
  • Include compliance in budget reviews, so the metric belongs to the business and not to procurement alone.

Card and T&E spend often sits with finance, outside procurement’s view. The distinction between spend management vs expense management clarifies which controls belong to which team.

Quick win this week: Send the business units with the highest maverick spend rates a one-page summary of their off-contract suppliers.

Tip 5: Monitor compliance and improve continuously

Step 5 of 5 · Measure

Manage maverick spend as a monthly metric, not a one-time project. Purchasing behavior shifts as categories, suppliers and teams change. A program that worked last year can quietly drift.

Track 6 KPIs:

  • maverick spend rate
  • contract-compliant spend
  • spend under management
  • share of invoices backed by a PO
  • catalog adoption
  • request-to-PO cycle time

Set spend-under-management targets against Ardent Partners' top-performer benchmark, where top performers often manage more than 90% of enterprise spend. Treat recurring exceptions as evidence of a catalog or contract gap, not a disciplinary issue. Review the purchasing policy quarterly, and bring under contract any category where requesters repeatedly bypass the system.

Quick win this week: Add the maverick spend rate to the monthly procurement and finance dashboard.

90-day action plan

For teams starting from scratch, the 5 tips translate into a practical first quarter:

1
Days 1–30: Establish the baseline

Run the maverick spend analysis across AP, P-card, T&E and ERP data. Output: a baseline maverick spend rate by category and business unit.

2
Days 31–60: Remove friction

Fill the top catalog gaps, launch guided buying for high-volume categories and publish a one-page policy. Output: fewer off-catalog requests.

3
Days 61–90: Assign ownership and measure

Introduce risk-based approvals, send monthly budget-owner reports and set KPI targets. Output: the first month-over-month trend in the maverick spend rate.

Frequently asked questions about maverick spend

What is maverick spend in procurement? +
Maverick spend is purchasing outside an organization’s agreed procurement process. Examples include buying off contract, using non-approved suppliers, skipping approvals or raising POs after the invoice arrives.
What are some examples of maverick spending? +
Typical examples are software subscriptions on corporate cards, a venue booked before a PO exists and orders split to avoid approval thresholds.
What causes maverick spend? +
Slow approvals, catalog gaps, low policy awareness and unmonitored card spend cause most maverick purchases. Employees usually bypass procurement to meet a deadline, not to break rules.
How can organizations identify maverick spend? +
Combine AP, P-card, T&E and ERP data, then flag non-PO invoices, off-contract prices, non-preferred suppliers and split orders. Divide the flagged spend by addressable spend to calculate the maverick spend rate.
How to reduce maverick spend in procurement? +
Make compliant buying faster than the workaround through guided buying and a single intake point. Then assign budget-owner accountability and review compliance KPIs every month.
What are the benefits of reducing maverick spend? +
The main benefits are realized contract savings, lower supplier risk, reliable spend data and stronger leverage at contract renewal.

Conclusion: Build better control over maverick spend

Maverick spend falls when the compliant buying route becomes the easiest route for every requester. Procurement leaders in Agiloft’s 2026 survey estimate that at least 15% of supplier spend flows off contract. Closing that gap is one of the most direct sources of recoverable value procurement has.

The fastest first move is the Tip 1 analysis. A baseline maverick spend rate shows where reducing maverick spend will pay back first. It also gives finance a number to track alongside budget performance.
