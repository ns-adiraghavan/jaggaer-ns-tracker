What Is SRM in Procurement?

SRM (supplier relationship management) is how procurement manages its most important suppliers to cut risk and add value beyond price. To define SRM in one line: rank suppliers by business impact, then invest time, data and collaboration where dependence is highest. SRM spans the source-to-pay cycle, from supplier onboarding and performance reviews to risk monitoring and contract renewal.

Its logic comes from Peter Kraljic’s 1983 Harvard Business Review article, “Purchasing Must Become Supply Management.” Kraljic told buyers to segment purchases by profit impact and supply risk and manage each segment differently. That focus on a few critical suppliers is what separates SRM from supplier management, which covers the whole supply base.

SRM Meaning: SRM vs Supplier Management and SCM

In procurement, supplier relationship management means the strategic layer of supplier management, reserved for the suppliers your business depends on most. Supplier management covers every supplier, while SRM goes deeper with the few that carry the most risk and value.

Discipline Scope Focus
Supplier Relationship Management Priority suppliers Long-term value, joint improvement and risk reduction
Supplier Management Entire supply base Onboarding, qualification, performance, risk and compliance
Vendor Management Contracted vendors, often IT and services Cost control, service levels and risk across the deal life cycle
Supply Chain Management End-to-end flow, from sourcing to delivery Planning sourcing, conversion and logistics across partners

Vendor management centers on contracts, and SCM centers on the flow of goods. SRM centers on relationships and decides which suppliers earn deeper engagement. That engagement serves three goals.

Goals of Supplier Relationship Management

Supplier relationship management has three goals: build strategic relationships, reduce supplier risk and create value beyond price.

Build strategic supplier relationships

Focus relationship effort on suppliers you cannot easily replace. An electronics maker’s microprocessor supplier is strategic but its paint supplier is not. Deeper supplier collaboration also pays off in resilience. In Deloitte’s 2025 CPO Survey, 61% of CPOs ranked supplier information sharing and collaboration among their most effective risk strategies.

Reduce supplier risk

Monitor strategic suppliers continuously, not only at annual reviews. Track financial health, delivery performance, geographic exposure and supplier compliance. Continuous monitoring is still rare: BSI found 54% of organizations manage supply chain risk mainly reactively. AI in supplier risk management helps close that gap by flagging early warning signals.

Create value beyond price

Measure supplier value in total cost, not unit price. Strategic suppliers can co-develop products, shorten lead times and support sustainability targets. These gains depend on multi-year commitments and shared plans, which one-off negotiations rarely deliver. Reaching all three goals takes a repeatable process.

The SRM Process in 4 Steps

Step 1: Segment suppliers

Segment suppliers by profit impact and supply risk using the Kraljic matrix and category strategy. Base the scoring on spend analytics and operational impact, not spend alone. A low-cost part can still halt a production line.

Quadrant Profit impact / Supply risk SRM treatment
Strategic High / High Partnership, joint plans, contingency planning
Leverage High / Low Competitive sourcing, volume consolidation
Bottleneck Low / High Secure supply, buffer stock, alternative sources
Non-critical Low / Low Automate and standardize buying

Step 2: Assign a strategy and owner to each segment

Match effort to the quadrant. Give every strategic supplier a named relationship owner, an executive sponsor and a joint business plan. Run non-critical suppliers through automated, low-touch processes.

Step 3: Measure performance

Score priority suppliers on five core KPIs: delivery, quality, cost, responsiveness and risk. Share scorecards with suppliers so both sides act on the same data. The supplier performance rating checklist sets out criteria to standardize scoring.

Step 4: Review and improve

Hold quarterly business reviews with strategic suppliers to agree corrective actions and next targets. Re-segment the supply base at least once a year, and after any major disruption. Kraljic’s matrix is a snapshot, and supply risk changes faster than annual plans. Run consistently, this cycle produces measurable benefits.

Benefits and Challenges of SRM

Supplier relationship management improves supply continuity, visibility, quality and innovation. Silos, cost-only thinking and shallow supplier visibility are what most often hold programs back.

Benefits

Supply continuity.

Strong relationships make backup sources faster to activate. In Deloitte’s survey, 74% of CPOs ranked active alternative sources as a top risk strategy.

Deeper visibility.

McKinsey’s 2025 risk survey found 95% of companies see tier-one supplier risk, but only 42% see tier two or beyond. Trusted tier-one partners are the route to that deeper view.

Better quality and delivery.

Shared scorecards turn performance gaps into joint corrective actions.

Supplier-led innovation.

Strategic suppliers bring new materials, designs and process ideas to the table earlier.

Challenges

  • ✓ Siloed data and teams. Siloed working is the top barrier to procurement value, cited by 57% of CPOs in Deloitte’s survey.
  • ✓ Cost-only focus. Programs judged only on savings erode the trust that strategic relationships depend on.
  • ✓ Limited multi-tier visibility. Many tier-one suppliers are reluctant to connect buyers with their own suppliers.
  • ✓ Changing supplier diversity rules. A March 2026 US executive order restricts DEI practices by federal contractors, and supplier diversity program design may fall within its scope. Executive support largely holds, but many programs are changing how they are positioned and measured.

The right software addresses several of these challenges directly.

SRM Software, Tools and Systems

An SRM system, or SRM tool, centralizes supplier data so teams can segment, score and monitor suppliers in one place. Look for four capabilities in any supplier relationship system:

  • ✓ A 360° supplier record covering performance, risk, ESG and financial data
  • ✓ Configurable scorecards that trigger corrective action plans automatically
  • ✓ Risk monitoring that combines internal data with external risk signals
  • ✓ Integration with ERP, sourcing and contract tools
