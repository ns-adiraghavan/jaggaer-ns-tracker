What Is the Procure to Pay Process?

Procure to pay (P2P) is the end-to-end process for requesting, approving, ordering, receiving, and paying for goods and services. It ends when the supplier is paid and the transaction is reconciled, which is why any useful procure to pay definition spans both procurement and finance.

The procure to pay business process connects 2 teams with different priorities. Procurement wants the right goods at the negotiated price. Accounts payable wants accurate invoices paid on time with a clean audit trail.

A connected procure to pay workflow gives both teams the same records. An approved order then moves to payment without being re-entered. P2P covers indirect spend, such as software and services, and direct spend, such as components and raw materials.

Procure to Pay vs. Source to Pay vs. Accounts Payable

ProcessWhat it coversWhere it starts
Source to pay (S2P)Spend analysis, sourcing, supplier selection, and contracting, plus every P2P stepCategory and sourcing decisions
Procure to pay (P2P)Requisition, approval, purchase order, receipt, invoice, matching, and paymentA business need
Accounts payable (AP)Invoice receipt, matching, approval, and paymentThe supplier invoice

P2P is the downstream half of S2P, and AP is the final stage of P2P.

The Procure to Pay Workflow at a Glance

ProcurementAccounts payable
1Need 2Requisition 3Approval 4Purchase order 5Receipt 6Invoice 7Match 8Payment
1
Identify the Need

A department needs a product or service. Guided buying points users to approved catalogs and preferred suppliers from the start.

2
Create the Purchase Requisition

The requester records the item, quantity, and cost center. Catalog items pre-fill prices and supplier details.

3
Approve the Requisition

Budget holders check the request against policy. Workflows route approvals by amount and category, with reminders.

4
Issue the Purchase Order

The approved request becomes a purchase order (PO) with agreed prices and terms. POs are created and sent electronically, and suppliers confirm them in a portal.

5
Receive Goods or Services

The receiving team confirms what arrived and its condition. Digital goods receipts are logged against the PO, including partial deliveries.

6
Capture the Supplier Invoice

Invoices arrive on paper, as PDFs, through portals, or as e-invoices. With accounts payable automation, invoices are captured across channels such as portal, EDI, and PEPPOL, and the data is extracted without manual keying.

7
Match the Invoice and Resolve Exceptions

The invoice is compared with the PO and goods receipt, known as three-way matching. Matching invoices pass straight through. Only price, quantity, or missing-PO exceptions reach a person.

8
Pay the Supplier and Reconcile

Approved invoices are paid on terms or early to capture discounts. Payments are then reconciled in the ERP automatically.

Why Manual P2P Processes Cost More

Higher Cost per Invoice

According to Ardent Partners' AP Metrics that Matter in 2026, the average invoice costs $9.90 and takes 11.6 days to process. Much of that cost comes from manual work, such as keying invoice data, chasing missing receipts, and resolving price or quantity mismatches. Each extra touch adds time, and slow processing can lead to missed early-payment discounts or late fees.

Procure to Pay Systems: Features and How to Choose

A procure to pay system is software that runs the full purchasing cycle in one place, from requisition and approval to purchase order, receipt, and invoice matching. It also handles supplier payment, applies buying rules automatically, and gives procurement and finance one shared record of every transaction.

Unified procure to pay systems remove the handoffs between steps, where most delays and errors happen.

Core Features of a P2P System

  • Guided buying: catalogs, punchouts, and contract pricing
  • Requisition and approval workflows: configurable rules by amount, category, and cost center
  • Supplier portal: order confirmations, invoice submission, and payment status
  • Invoice capture and matching: multi-channel capture with two- and three-way matching
  • Payments and reconciliation: electronic payments and ERP posting
  • Supplier management and analytics: one supplier record plus spend reporting

Procure to Pay Automation and AI

Procure to pay automation began with digital workflows, then rules automated approval routing, matching, and payment scheduling. AI now handles work that fixed rules cannot.

Where AI Adds Value in P2P

  • Invoice processing: extracting and validating data from invoices in any format
  • Fraud and anomaly detection: flagging duplicate invoices, unusual prices, and bank-detail changes before payment
  • Predictive insight: warning of delivery delays or supplier disruptions early
  • User guidance: answering policy questions in plain language
  • AI agents: investigating a match exception, gathering context, and resolving or escalating it within set rules

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the procure to pay process? +
It is the full cycle an organization uses to buy goods and services. It runs from the first purchase request to the final supplier payment and its reconciliation in the ERP.
What are the steps in the P2P process? +
The 8 steps are: identify the need, create the requisition, approve it, and issue the purchase order. Then receive the goods, capture the invoice, match it, and pay and reconcile.
What is the difference between procure to pay and source to pay? +
Source to pay is the wider cycle. It starts with deciding what to buy and from whom, then negotiating contracts. Procure to pay covers the buying and payment that follow.
What is the difference between procure to pay and accounts payable? +
Accounts payable covers the invoice and payment stages. Procure to pay also includes the requisition, approval, purchase order, and receiving steps that come before the invoice.
What is three-way matching? +
Three-way matching compares the supplier invoice with the purchase order and the goods receipt before payment. Differences outside set tolerances are flagged as exceptions for review.
