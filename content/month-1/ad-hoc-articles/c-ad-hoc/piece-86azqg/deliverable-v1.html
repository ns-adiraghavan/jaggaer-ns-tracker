What is spend analytics?

Procurement spend analytics is the standing capability that keeps your view of spend current. It collects, cleanses, classifies and analyzes spend data, then feeds KPIs, dashboards and sourcing decisions.

Spend analytics in procurement also supports wider spend management. It supplies the intelligence that spend management best practices depend on across sourcing, contracts and purchasing. Run spend analytics software inside your source-to-pay platform, and findings flow straight into sourcing events and renewals.

The Hackett Group’s glossary frames spend analytics as an ongoing view of what you spend. Jaggaer’s article on what is spend analysis covers the underlying review process.

Spend optimization is the other half: analytics shows what happened, and optimization acts on it. Typical actions cover cost reduction, compliance and supplier performance, plus supplier consolidation, diversification and sustainability targets.

Why visibility and control matter in 2026

Contract-compliant spend averages 78.2%, according to Ardent’s 2026 Metrics report. Ardent describes the spend outside negotiated agreements as real financial leakage, and you can’t recover what you can’t see. Visibility comes first.

Cost and waste show up in Finance’s numbers first, and the cause stays hidden. Procurement spend analytics supplies the missing transparency: it points to specific pockets of unmanaged spend. Once you have spend visibility, you can act on causes and lower the chance that the same problem returns.

Eliminating dark and maverick spend

Maverick spend breaks policy, dark spend hides from procurement and tail spend is a long list of small purchases. Pin down your definitions before you set targets.

  • Maverick spend is a purchase made outside established procurement policy, such as with a non-approved supplier or around a contracted vendor. The path the purchase took defines it.
  • Dark spend is spend that procurement can’t see or attribute to a supplier, category or budget owner. Visibility defines it. The 2 terms overlap in everyday use.
  • Tail spend is the long list of small-value purchases that add up to a small share of total spend. Size defines it. Left alone, it becomes unmanaged tail spend.

One purchase can be all 3. Typical examples include travel booked outside approved tools, ad-hoc software subscriptions on corporate cards, office supplies bought on personal cards, and unapproved catering or event costs. The motive is usually speed, and usually innocent. Still, off-channel buying creates compliance exposure and weakens your hand in negotiations.

These purchases stay hidden because the data arrives late and unclassified. A card feed or an expense claim shows up after the event, with no link to a supplier, category or budget center. AI classifiers can infer missing detail from merchant codes or text, so review inferred categories before you report on them. That’s retrospective visibility, not proactive control.

Advanced spend analytics platforms narrow the gap. They can aggregate non-PO transactions and card data, and match supplier names and merchant codes against approved lists. They can also flag anomalies such as duplicate vendors, off-contract rates and out-of-hours purchases. And they can chart visible versus dark spend by business unit or category. Check that every flag traces back to its source transaction.

Once exceptions are visible, you can prevent and control maverick spend at the source, and enforcement becomes more credible and less punitive. Call it soft enforcement through data: people see that you spot exceptions, and behavior can change. You can also adjust policy to match. Require software subscriptions on corporate cards, and route pre-subscription approvals through your P2P workflow before booking. Dashboards can notify managers when off-channel subscriptions appear, and forecasting can flag likely hotspots, such as project teams with frequent last-minute bookings.

Which spend analytics KPIs should you track?

Track 6 KPIs: spend under management, maverick buying rate, contract utilization, price compliance, emergency purchases and PO cycle time. Define each one’s numerator and denominator before you compare it with anyone else’s number. Ardent’s 2026 Metrics report put total spend under management at 69.3% across participating organizations. That figure only helps if your base matches theirs.

Bases differ more than you might expect. AHRMM, the healthcare supply chain association, excludes taxes, salaries and bonuses, among other items, from total spend in its 2023 spend under management KPI.

OMB’s Memorandum M-19-13 (2019) sorts US federal spend into 4 maturity tiers, from Tier 0 (unaligned) to Tier 3 (Best in Class).

Deloitte’s 2025 Global CPO Survey estimates a related measure, spend influence, at 80% for its Digital Masters, the top-quartile performers, and 65% for Followers.

For spend analytics in procurement, metrics differ by sector and geography, but these 6 apply to almost every large, complex organization. Read them together. A high spend under management figure with low price compliance means contracts exist but buyers often ignore the agreed prices.

Worked example

AHRMM’s 2023 spend under management KPI divides spend actively sourced or managed by supply chain by total supply and purchased services expense. In its calculation, $600 million managed out of $1 billion gives a SUM of 60%.

Cost savings vs. cost avoidance: agree the baseline first

A saving only counts when Finance accepts the baseline. Cost savings reduce current spend. Cost avoidance prevents a future cost, which makes it forward-looking and harder to quantify. Classification varies by organization, so agree with Finance which items count as soft savings before you report them.

Then prove the number in 3 steps:

  1. Set the baseline before the negotiation: the previous price multiplied by forecast volume.
  2. Record the negotiated saving at award.
  3. Have Finance validate realized savings against actual invoiced spend.

Savings survive a budget review once Finance has validated them as realized.

Supplier performance metrics

Start with on-time delivery, defect rate and cost competitiveness. Then add lead-time variability, order accuracy and compliance rate. Because supplier performance has many dimensions, a dashboard serves you better than a spreadsheet. In Deloitte’s 2025 survey, 84% of Digital Masters met or exceeded their supplier performance plans, against 59% of Followers.

Fix the data before you trust the dashboard

Dashboards inherit the quality of the data behind them. Only 11% of organizations ran a unified procurement data model across source-to-pay in early 2026, according to Ardent Partners’ The Path to AI-First Procurement. But 41% had integrated data they reconciled by hand, 40% worked from fragmented data and 8% kept key intelligence trapped in documents and emails.

Data defects flow straight into KPIs. One supplier can end up as 3 records when duplicates go unmerged. Uncategorized card spend lands in “miscellaneous.” Unlinked PO, invoice and contract data hides maverick spend.

Data quality and structure tops the AI barriers in both of Ardent’s 2026 reports, at 59% in each. In the 2026 AI-First report that is 59% of respondents. In the 2026 Metrics report it is 59% of organizations already working with AI.

In ProcureAbility’s 2026 CPO Benchmark Study, 51% of the 160 senior procurement leaders surveyed named data quality as their biggest analytics gap. In Deloitte’s 2025 survey, data quality ranks first among internal generative AI risks, at 44%.

Before you buy dashboards or AI, confirm 4 basics:

  • Supplier master: one record per supplier, with a named owner who approves new entries.
  • Category taxonomy: a single list that finance, procurement and business units all report against.
  • Record linkage: PO, invoice, card and contract lines that resolve to the same supplier and category.
  • Feedback loop: analyst corrections that carry forward into the next classification run.

Dashboards built on unreconciled data look polished and can mislead. Fixing the data takes longer than buying a tool, and it decides whether anyone trusts the numbers.

Turn analytics into negotiating leverage

Spend analytics earns its keep at the negotiating table, where facts replace haggling. These 4 uses matter most:

  1. Internal benchmarking. Compare prices for identical or equivalent items across business units, plants or regions. In a hypothetical example, safety gloves cost €1.10 per pair at one plant and €0.85 at another. That gap is room to harmonize before you open the conversation.
  2. External benchmarking. Compare against third-party market data or index-linked commodity prices. That gives you a market baseline to test the supplier’s quote against.
  3. Total cost of ownership. Compare suppliers on real cost. Add direct costs such as price, freight and duties to indirect costs such as quality failures, late deliveries and service calls, plus penalties, warranty claims and non-conformance costs. Then allocate them per supplier or contract.
  4. Post-negotiation validation. Track actual against negotiated prices in your P2P system and flag deviations: off-contract buys, price creep and invoice mismatches. Savings you negotiate but don’t verify erode quietly.

Volume consolidation, performance-based terms and alternative pricing models build on the same data. Start with these 4.

How to build a spend analytics strategy

A spend analytics strategy settles 5 questions in order: what you need to know, where the data lives, how you classify it, which KPIs prove progress and who acts. Whatever spend analytics technology you choose, those 5 questions stay the same.

Step 1
Set the questions

Write down the decisions the analysis must support, such as which categories to consolidate or which buying channels to enforce. Every later step serves those decisions.

Step 2
Connect the sources

Bring in procurement, ERP, finance, P-card, T&E, supplier master, contract and invoice data. Remove duplicates and normalize formats until finance and procurement agree on one set of numbers.

Step 3
Classify and enrich

Group transactions by category, business unit and region with AI/ML or rule-based mapping. Then add supplier risk, ESG scores, payment terms and market indices.

Step 4
Track the KPIs

Put the 6 KPIs above on dashboards cut by supplier, category, region and trend. Review exceptions on a fixed cadence and give each one an owner.

Step 5
Act and prove it

Feed findings into sourcing events and contract renewals, then report 3 numbers: the baseline, the negotiated saving and the Finance-validated saving. What you learn reshapes step 1.

Agree how you will measure success before you deploy anything. Clear and proven ROI is the top accelerator of AI adoption in Ardent’s 2026 AI-First report, cited by 59% of respondents.

Spend analytics software and AI

AI in spend analytics: recent developments

According to Ardent Partners’ The Path to AI-First Procurement (2026), spend analytics leads current AI adoption, at 22% in use and 58% planned within 12 months. Before you buy spend analytics software or compare spend analytics tools, know which AI you are buying.

Deloitte’s 2025 Global CPO Survey ranks spend analytics and dashboarding first among generative AI use cases, at 53%, ahead of RFx generation at 42%. Its CPOs also name data analytics the top area of next-generation technology adoption, at 88%.

In EY’s 2025 survey of about 75 CPOs, 80% plan to deploy generative AI within 3 years, starting with spend analytics and insights. Just 36% had it deployed in a meaningful way.

43% of organizations are actively pursuing AI deployment, nearly twice the 2025 share, according to Hackett’s 2026 Procurement Key Issues Study. For the first time, AI-enabled technology ranks among the top 3 procurement priorities.

Ardent Partners’ The Path to AI-First Procurement (2026) cautions that much of the AI in spend analytics in 2026 is established machine learning and rules-based capability. Generative and agentic capabilities were mostly still ahead of the market. Useful, yes, but established. So ask which AI you are buying, what it does and how you check it.
