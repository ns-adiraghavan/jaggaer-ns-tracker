Start with the definition

What Is Spend Analysis?

Spend analysis is the process of collecting, cleansing, classifying and analyzing an organization’s spend data to cut costs, control risk and enforce contracts. It shows what you buy, from which suppliers, at what price and on which terms.

Connect every source before you analyze any of it. Spend analysis in procurement joins accounts payable, general ledger, purchasing card and contract data into one view by supplier and category. Without that join, each team argues from its own extract.

Most teams still decide on a fraction of the data they hold. McKinsey estimates that procurement functions use less than 20% of the data available to them to support decision-making (2026). Spend analysis brings more of that data into the decision.

Question spend analysis answersData it draws on
What are we spending, and with whom?Accounts payable invoices, general ledger, purchasing cards
Are we buying on contract?Contracts, PO lines, invoice prices
Which suppliers are really one supplier?Supplier master, parent-child hierarchies
Where do cost and risk concentrate?Category taxonomy, country of origin, supplier risk data

Keep the definition narrow: spend analysis finds the opportunity, and sourcing, contracts and buying controls capture it. That split keeps each team accountable for its share of the savings.

Use the same data as a single source of truth beyond procurement. Finance can budget and plan from it, operations can manage inventory against it, and category teams can set a strategic sourcing roadmap.

Quick answers

Spend Analysis FAQs

What is the difference between spend analysis and spend analytics?+
Spend analysis is the process of collecting, cleansing, classifying and analyzing spend data. Spend analytics usually refers to the software and dashboards that run that process continuously. Many procurement teams use the two terms interchangeably.
Can AI analyze procurement spend?+
AI can classify spend transactions, flag duplicate suppliers and surface unusual purchases for review. It needs clean supplier and category data to do that well. Keep a person approving each action, and ask the tool to show the source behind every answer.
What is tail spend analysis?+
Tail spend analysis reviews the many low-value purchases spread across a long list of suppliers. It shows where you can consolidate suppliers, add catalogs or move repeat buying onto existing contracts.
What is an example of spend analysis?+
Illustrative example: a manufacturer links 3 supplier name variants to 1 parent company. It then finds that parent supplies 4 plants on 4 different price lists. Moving all 4 plants onto one contract is the next step.
What are the benefits of spend analysis?+
Spend analysis gives procurement one trusted source of spend data. Teams use it to bring more spend under management, improve contract compliance, shorten cycle times and uncover maverick spend. It also gives the CPO and CFO numbers they can defend with executives.
How often should you run a spend analysis?+
Run spend analysis continuously rather than once a year. Refresh the data at least as often as you close the books. Set alerts for new suppliers, off-contract buying and price changes between reviews.
Seven steps, run continuously

How the Spend Analysis Process Works

Run spend analysis in 7 steps: identify, gather, cleanse, group, categorize, analyze and repeat. Then keep the cycle running, so every category review starts from current data rather than last year’s extract.

Measure the gap the process closes. Ardent Partners’ July 2026 research found 48% of procurement organizations still work from fragmented or largely unstructured data. Closing that gap starts with seeing all of the spend.

1
Identify every source

List accounts payable, general ledger, purchasing card, eProcurement and contract data, plus the supplier master. Add customs and country-of-origin records if you import.

2
Gather it in one place

Pull every source into one data set through connectors or ERP feeds rather than manual extracts.

3
Cleanse to one standard

Correct descriptions, currencies and duplicate records. Hand routine fixes to AI-assisted cleansing, and keep a person reviewing the exceptions.

4
Group suppliers by parent

Link name variants and subsidiaries to one parent supplier. Then enrich each record with risk and diversity attributes for supplier management.

5
Categorize against one taxonomy

Choose UNSPSC, ECLASS or your own taxonomy, then classify every line against it.

6
Analyze against KPIs

Track spend under management, contract-compliant spend, savings and suppliers per category.

7
Repeat continuously

Refresh the data on a schedule. Set alerts for new suppliers, off-contract buying and price changes.

Spend Analysis Trends for 2027

All 5 spend analysis trends for 2027 turn on data readiness: semantics, role redesign, AI priority, tariffs and digitization. Each one rewards teams whose spend data is already clean and classified.

1
Semantics decide how well AI agents perform

Gartner predicts that by 2027, organizations that prioritize semantics in AI-ready data will raise agentic AI accuracy by up to 80% (2026). The same organizations will cut costs by up to 60%. Spend analysis starts that work with consistent category and supplier definitions.

2
AI expectations outrun role redesign

Two Gartner surveys, concluded in April 2026 and published on October 8, point the same way. 63% of senior procurement leaders expect AI to significantly improve performance over the next 3 years. But only 25% of procurement leaders have redesigned jobs and roles as a direct result of AI.

3
AI joins the top 3 procurement priorities

Hackett’s 2026 study ranks deploying AI-enabled technology among the top 3 procurement priorities for the first time, behind supply continuity and cost reduction. Hackett also found 43% actively pursuing AI-enabled technology, nearly double last year’s level.

4
Tariffs keep reshaping the supply base

The 2026 AlixPartners Disruption Index found 82% of executives have adjusted, or are adjusting, their supply chains in response to tariffs and geopolitical instability.

5
Digitization targets run to 2027

AlixPartners’ 2026 CPO Survey found almost half of procurement executives cite digitization as their primary value lever. Their goal is to digitize up to 70% of procurement processes by 2027. Its October 2026 cost outlook expects IT hardware and infrastructure to sustain double-digit inflation through 2027.

Expect some agent projects to stop before they scale. Gartner predicts organizations will cancel over 40% of agentic AI projects by the end of 2027 (2025). Gartner names escalating costs, unclear business value or inadequate risk controls as the reasons.

What Changed in 2026

Recheck tariff exposure in your spend data, because the legal basis for US tariffs changed in 2026. 3 changes matter for spend analysis.

1
The Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs (20 February 2026)

AlixPartners reports that a 6-3 ruling in Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump found IEEPA does not authorize tariffs. CBP opened its CAPE refund portal on 20 April 2026 and began issuing refunds in May.

2
A temporary Section 122 surcharge ran and expired (24 February to 24 July 2026)

Proclamation 11012 imposed a 10% surcharge for 150 days. On 7 May 2026, the Court of International Trade held it invalid. Relief went only to the plaintiffs, and an appeal is pending.

3
Section 301 forced-labor tariffs took over (24 July 2026)

USTR imposed additional duties of 10% or 12.5% on imports from 60 economies. Section 232 duties on steel, aluminum, copper and other sectors continue.

Size refund eligibility and new exposure from the same data set. AlixPartners advises auditing IEEPA-related tariff payments made since 2025 by category and supplier. Extend the same audit to Section 122 payments, which may also be recoverable while the appeal runs. Spend analysis that already tags supplier and country of origin makes that audit a query, not a project.

Spend Analysis Strategies That Work

Point spend analysis at 3 places where savings hide: tariff exposure, the long tail and off-contract buying. Each one needs the same clean supplier and category data, so build that data once and use it for all 3.

Size Tariff Exposure

Cut spend by supplier, category and country of origin to size tariff exposure and refund eligibility. Add customs entries to the data set, so each tariff payment ties back to a supplier and a contract.

Map exposure before the next announcement. AlixPartners’ October 2026 outlook says the companies pulling ahead have mapped exposure by product, supplier geography and trade flow. They built mitigation options rather than reacting to each announcement.

3–5%

AlixPartners found supplier cost increases averaged 5–7% over the past 12 months for most organizations. But leading procurement functions held them to 3–5%.

Source: AlixPartners CPO Executive Survey, 2026. Leading functions apply a wider set of mitigation levers beyond direct negotiation and RFPs. AlixPartners credits that to spend visibility, category intelligence and analytical capacity.

Illustrative example: discrete manufacturing

Tag every direct-material line with country of origin before the next tariff change. A manufacturer buying castings from 3 countries can then compare landed cost by supplier. It can also see which contracts carry tariff clauses and model a switch before it renegotiates.

Tail Spend Analysis

Use tail spend analysis to find low-value suppliers you can consolidate, catalog or move onto contract. Start with suppliers used once or twice a year in categories where you already hold a contract.

McKinsey reports a telco that used AI agents in negotiations across its long-tail software spend, cutting analysis and email time by up to 90% (2026). McKinsey reports the AI-guided negotiations led to 10–15% savings across suppliers. Test the approach on your own tail first, with a person signing off each agreed price.

Bring Off-Contract Spend Back

Route repeat off-contract purchases, the maverick spend in your data, to existing contracts before you negotiate new ones. Spend analysis shows which suppliers already hold a contract. The fix is often a catalog or a buying rule, not a new sourcing event.

Multi-source integration

Test it: ask for a live feed from your ERP, accounts payable and card data, not a sample file.

2

Cleansing and normalization

Gartner’s required features include removing duplicates, correcting errors and normalizing currencies across regions.

Test it: load a supplier list with name variants and mixed currencies. Count the manual fixes.

3

Data enrichment

Gartner expects tools to enrich spend data with supplier details, market intelligence and custom taxonomies.

Test it: ask how the tool maps your own taxonomy alongside UNSPSC.

4

Role-based dashboards

Gartner lists customizable dashboards and reports for each user role as a required feature.

Test it: ask a category manager to build a view without help from IT.

5

AI with governance

Ardent’s July 2026 research found 63% of procurement leaders name human-in-the-loop approval as the non-negotiable AI governance requirement.

Test it: ask the AI a spend question. Check that it shows the source data behind the answer.

6

One data layer

McKinsey calls for a common data spine across spend, suppliers, contracts and market benchmarks (2026).

Test it: change a contract price, then check whether the spend view picks it up.
