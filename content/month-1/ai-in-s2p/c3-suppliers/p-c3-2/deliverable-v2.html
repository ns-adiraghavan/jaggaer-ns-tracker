What a Sourcing Brief Needs Before Claude Can Use It

Claude can't invent context it doesn't have. Hand it these five things first, and there's far less rework waiting:

InputWhat it does
Category contextWhat's being sourced, and why now
Evaluation weightsWhich criteria matter, and how much each counts
Minimum requirementsThe non-negotiables that disqualify a response outright
Response structureHow suppliers organize submissions, so they're easy to compare
TimelineIssue date, question deadline, submission date, award date

Most rfp creation software asks a category manager to enter this same information manually. JAGGAER's Sourcing module skips that: the information is already structured at intake, ready for Claude.

Skip any of the five and Claude fills the gap with a guess. Evaluation weights are riskiest to skip. Get those wrong, and you've shaped who wins before a supplier even responds.

The Evaluation Framework Is the Part That Actually Matters

Get cost-weighting wrong in a vendor evaluation, and it shows up later, in failures, disputes, and a vendor asking why they lost.

The draft RFP isn't what causes outcomes like that; the scoring framework behind it is. Claude writes strong RFP language in minutes, but building a framework that ties every requirement to a weighted score takes longer.

Because the sourcing brief already sets evaluation criteria and weights, Claude carries that structure straight into the draft. Scores map back to exactly what the document asked suppliers to provide.

JAGGAER's own research treats this as a requirement: scoring logic must stay transparent, with a full audit trail of inputs, weights, and changes. Four things make an evaluation defensible:

  • Disclose criteria to every vendor before proposals arrive
  • Document weights before scoring begins
  • Apply the same rubric consistently across every evaluator
  • Justify the final decision against the documented scores

This kind of ai bid management only works if scores stay tied to the criteria the RFP published, not how a reviewer felt about a proposal. Get the framework right alongside the draft, and it defends itself later, with no scrambling when a vendor asks why they lost.

A strong framework still runs on judgment. Here's where that judgment doesn't move.

What Claude Doesn't Decide For You

Three things stay with the procurement professional regardless of how good the draft is: sourcing strategy, supplier market knowledge, and the commercial conversation once responses land. Claude doesn't decide which suppliers get invited, whether an event should be single-source or competitive, or what the market will bear on price. Claude doesn't replace procurement, per JAGGAER's own research; strategic decisions, supplier relationships, and risk trade-offs still need a person in the loop.

A fourth risk sits underneath all three: Claude can occasionally invent plausible-sounding supplier details, including fabricated ISO certifications, according to JAGGAER's own testing. Every AI-generated supplier list needs a human check first.

The negotiation after the RFP closes (price, terms, final award) stays human from start to finish. JAI suggests. You decide. Always.

That principle carries extra weight in regulated sourcing, where JAI is trained on the audit-trail documentation required at each decision point. An AI-drafted RFP still needs a procurement review before it goes out, especially in regulated categories.

FAQ

What does Claude need from a sourcing brief to draft an RFP? +
Category context, evaluation weights, minimum requirements, response structure, timeline. Miss any, and Claude assumes. That's risky for evaluation criteria.
How much of the draft RFP is usable without editing? +
Structure and standard language, like cover pages and boilerplate terms, need only light edits. Category-specific requirements and weights still need a professional's review.
Does Claude also produce an evaluation framework, or just the RFP document? +
Both, if asked. Claude can produce a weighted scoring structure alongside the RFP, though it's only as reliable as your brief's weights.
What still needs a procurement professional's judgment? +
Sourcing strategy, supplier market knowledge, and the commercial conversation after the RFP closes. Claude drafts the document, not your supplier relationships or negotiating room.
Is this different from using Claude to draft an RFI or RFQ? +
Yes. An RFP compares approaches with multi-dimensional criteria, not just price. An RFI scans the market before a formal process. An RFQ compares price with little evaluation weight.
Is this suitable for public sector or regulated RFPs? +
With review, yes. Public sector and regulated sourcing carry compliance and audit requirements a first draft won't know unless the brief specifies them. Sign-off before issue isn't optional here.
How is this different from writing my own Claude prompt for an RFP? +
A prompt from scratch restates category context, requirements, and weights every time. A brief captured once in JAGGAER's Sourcing module sits ready, and nothing needs retyping.
Can Claude help evaluate supplier responses once they come in? +
Yes, as a first pass. JAGGAER's Sourcing module shows this live: JAI scores RFQ responses against weighted criteria and recommends a supplier, though only as a starting point.
