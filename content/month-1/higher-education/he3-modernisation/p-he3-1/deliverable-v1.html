The Problem: Why Spreadsheets Break Down at R1 Scale in 2026

At most R1 universities, procurement money moves through dozens of disconnected departmental workflows instead of one coordinated process. According to NACUBO's Spendmetric initiative, that money is the second-largest line item in a typical institution's own budget. It generally runs 20% to 33% of total spend, trailing only salaries and benefits. That's a share of one institution's budget, not the national total. Across all degree-granting institutions, the National Center for Education Statistics puts combined spending at $702 billion in 2020–21, the most recent year NCES has published.

Sponsored research adds another layer. According to the National Science Foundation's Higher Education R&D Survey, U.S. universities spent $108.8 billion on R&D in FY2023, with federal sources funding $59.7 billion, or 55%, of that total. That's the scale of federally funded activity every one of these institutions' procurement teams has to keep compliant.

According to the Council on Governmental Relations's 2024-2025 Year in Review, the organization represents 232 member institutions and tracks federal procurement standards under 2 CFR 200.317–327 closely on their behalf. Those standards set the dollar lines a purchase must follow. Procedures stay informal below the micro-purchase threshold, $15,000 as of October 2025. Above the $350,000 simplified acquisition threshold, formal competitive bidding kicks in.

Every purchase tied to a federal grant already has to satisfy those standards, on top of whatever the university's own policy requires. That compliance load touches the governance side of R1 procurement modernization, which this piece treats as a separate conversation from the operational fix covered here.

What to Look for in a Platform

Evaluating platforms comes down to 5 criteria: spend visibility, supplier management, approval workflows, compliance and audit trails, and AI-assisted sourcing. A Canadian university running an AI-enabled RFP process in 2026 checks the same five boxes as an R1 institution in the U.S.; the criteria don't change by geography. Whatever the location, R1 university procurement platform modernisation succeeds or fails on how well a platform handles these five areas together, not any one in isolation.

  • Spend visibility: real-time reporting across departments and campuses, with drill-down by department, grant, and supplier, not just a total dollar figure at the end of the quarter.
  • Supplier management: a single supplier record per vendor across departments, with contract terms and shared discount tiers earned once, across the institution.
  • Approval workflows: routing tied to dollar amount, commodity, and cost center, so a $200 lab order and a $2 million equipment purchase follow different paths.
  • Compliance and audit trails: automatic logging tied to federal procurement standards under 2 CFR 200, since sponsored-research purchases need to hold up to audit.
  • AI-assisted sourcing: according to a 2025 EDUCAUSE QuickPoll, 63% of procurement teams already account for AI products in procurement, or plan to. The top two challenges were the pace of AI's change (45%) and insufficient AI governance (40%). That governance gap matters more at R1 scale, since a single ungoverned AI tool touching sponsored-research data creates federal compliance exposure beyond ordinary vendor risk. Look for AI sourcing recommendations that stay grounded in your own policies and data, cited to source, with a human decision-maker in the loop on every award.

None of these five criteria work in isolation. A platform that nails spend visibility but skips AI governance still leaves procurement teams doing manual review on the fastest-growing risk category in higher ed.

Getting Started: What to Evaluate First

Before evaluating any higher education procurement software vendor as part of an R1 university procurement platform modernisation effort, get honest answers to 3 questions:

  • 1.Current spend visibility: what does it actually look like today, department by department, not just the aggregate level finance already tracks?
  • 2.ERP integration requirements: which system version is running, and who owns that relationship internally?
  • 3.Departmental buy-in: where is it weakest, since a migration stalls fastest in the department that never agreed it was a problem?

Answering those honestly, before a vendor conversation starts, is what separates a smooth rollout from a stalled one. FAQ

Why is spreadsheet-based procurement especially risky at large research universities? +
Spreadsheets and email have no audit trail, so a single missed approval or duplicate order is hard to catch until it shows up in a budget review. At R1 scale, that risk multiplies across dozens of departments and every sponsored-research grant subject to federal compliance rules under 2 CFR 200.
Does modernizing procurement require replacing our existing ERP system? +
No. Platforms like JAGGAER connect to 40+ ERP and finance systems, including Oracle, SAP, Workday, and Microsoft Dynamics, through prebuilt connectors. The ERP stays in place; the procurement layer changes.
What should an R1 university look for in a procurement platform? +
Look for 5 criteria: spend visibility, supplier management, approval workflows, compliance and audit trails, and AI-assisted sourcing with governance built in. A platform that's strong on one but weak on the others still leaves gaps.
What role does AI play in modern university procurement platforms? +
According to a 2025 EDUCAUSE QuickPoll, 63% of procurement teams already account for AI products in their processes, or plan to. The top challenge cited was keeping pace with how fast AI tools change, with insufficient AI governance close behind. AI-assisted sourcing should come with a named control: recommendations grounded in policy, cited to source, with a human making the final call.
What should we evaluate first before starting a modernization project? +
Start with 3 things: what current spend visibility actually looks like department by department, what your ERP integration requirements are, and where departmental buy-in is weakest. Vendor conversations go faster once those three answers are honest ones.
What is the actual ROI of moving from spreadsheets to a unified platform? +
There is no single ROI percentage that applies across every R1 institution, since it depends on starting spend visibility, adoption, and implementation scope. Calculate it as hard savings plus cost avoidance plus labor capacity released plus compliance value, minus program cost, divided by program cost. Track that against a real baseline: current off-catalog spend, invoice processing time, contract compliance, and staff hours spent on manual transaction work, not a generic industry benchmark.
How long does a procurement platform modernization typically take? +
There is no fixed timeline, since duration depends on 3 factors: how many ERP and finance systems need to connect, how many departments and campuses are in scope, and how much historical supplier and contract data needs to migrate. A single-campus rollout onto an existing ERP connector is a materially shorter project than a multi-campus effort covering sponsored-research purchasing across dozens of departments. Ask any vendor for a timeline based on your specific ERP, department count, and data migration scope, not a generic industry average.
